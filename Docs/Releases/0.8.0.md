# Strata 0.8.0

Strata is a 3D game engine written in C++20, with an editor that people and AI agents drive through the same commands,
game logic written as C++ scripts that the editor builds, and exported games that run in StrataRuntime. 0.8.0 holds
what development wave 8 delivered, landed on 2026-11-23, on top of everything in [0.7.0][notes-0.7.0] (wave 7),
[0.6.0][notes-0.6.0] (wave 6), [0.5.0][notes-0.5.0] (wave 5), [0.4.0][notes-0.4.0] (wave 4) and
[0.3.0][notes-0.3.0] (waves 1 to 3). Versions follow the waves (0.9.0 will be wave 9), and patch versions carry fixes.

`SHA256SUMS.txt` holds the checksums of all three. To verify an archive, run `sha256sum -c <archive>.sha256` on Linux,
`shasum -a 256 -c <archive>.sha256` on macOS, or compare `(Get-FileHash <archive>).Hash` with the `.sha256` file in

Each archive holds one folder, `Strata-0.8.0-<platform>`. Unpack it anywhere (on Windows, see
[First start](#first-start) first) and start `StrataEditor` in it. Its `README.md` explains the rest: making a game and
connecting an AI agent.

Projects made with 0.7.0 open in 0.8.0 unchanged. Scenes that do not turn on the new features look much as they did,
with a few differences at High and Ultra: the sun's shadows are drawn by stable cascades, contact shadows are off, the
sun's highlight takes the sun's size and moving bodies are drawn between their physics steps (see
[Upgrading from 0.7.0](#upgrading-from-070)). Scripts build against the same SDK, which only gained functions; export
games again to get 0.8.0's runtime.

## What's new in 0.8.0

Wave 8 took on light: highlights on metal and wet ground, many lamps with shadows, light from glowing surfaces and light
that bounces, a showcase to show them, and motion that stays smooth at any frame rate.

- **A showcase sample: the High Desert Outpost.** A derelict fuel stop in a rocky desert valley the day after rain,
  first in the launcher's samples: a bus with bare and painted sheet steel, a weathered oil tank, wet asphalt with
  puddles, a hut, a fire and lanterns, made through the editor from 22 CC0 scans of Poly Haven and committed generators.
  Press Play for a camera tour of about 80 seconds through six shots at dawn, golden hour and dusk, each time of day with
  its own baked reflection probes; any key takes the tour over into a first-person walk, Tab flies, P opens a photo mode
  (time of day, exposure, field of view, focus, f-number, the quality preset and anti-aliasing, seven bookmarks), H
  hides the UI.
- **Global illumination (experimental).** The Post Process's **Global Illumination** (off by default, and off in every
  sample) adds bounce light traced in the world around the camera with radiance cascades, never from the screen: a
  sunlit floor lights the walls and the ceiling, a red floor tints them, glowing surfaces light their surroundings, light
  bounces on between surfaces at High, closed rooms stay dark, and moving the sun moves the bounce in the same frame.
  Nothing is blended over frames. Objects that move take part while they move. Graphics Quality has a row for it (Off at
  Low and Medium, High at High and Ultra; 25 MiB of GPU memory per view at Low, 156 MiB at High, only in scenes that turn
  it on). It is experimental: read its [known issues](#global-illumination-experimental) before you use it, and use it
  for interiors. `Docs/Manual/Lighting.md` explains it.
- **Rect lights.** A new light, the **Rect Light** (Hierarchy: Create > Rect Light): a glowing rectangle (a ceiling
  panel, a sign, a softbox) whose diffuse light is exact and whose highlight has its shape (linearly transformed cosines,
  Heitz et al. 2016): a polished floor mirrors the panel with crisp edges, wet ground draws a streak, rough metal a soft
  glow, with nothing sampled and nothing that needs TAA. Its shadows are soft and contact-hardening (Cast Shadows; at
  most four rect lights cast shadows at once). **Make Area Light** (the Hierarchy's context menu) makes an emissive
  panel a real light in one step and tags it **Light Surface**, so that reflections, probes and its own shadows do not
  count its glow twice. Scenes without rect lights render as before, bit for bit.
- **Many lamps with shadows.** 64 point and spot lights cast shadows at High and 256 at Ultra (16 at Medium and 4 at Low,
  as before; Custom any number the atlas holds). The shadows go to the lights that matter most and fade in and out over
  0.2 s, so they no longer pop or blink while the camera moves; a light whose shadow map changes size keeps its old
  shadow until the new one is drawn; their filter noise no longer turns from frame to frame under TAA. In a town of 256
  lanterns at 3840x2160 and Ultra their shadows add 0.07 ms to the opaque pass.
- **Sun shadows that hold still.** The sun's shadows are final in the first frame after the camera turns, moves or cuts,
  and nothing in the scene (a ball thrown past the camera) moves them: stable cascaded shadow maps, fixed splits from
  1.5 m to the Shadow Distance, snapped to whole texels. High and Ultra buy the detail back with larger maps (High 4096
  texels below 1440 output rows and 8192 from them, Ultra 8192) and a fifth cascade within 1.5 m of the camera, so
  close-ups keep fine shadows. Long shadows' soft tips are the same width in every cascade.
- **Highlights like in a drawing.** The sun's highlight takes the sun's size (the Directional Light's **Angular
  Diameter**): an overcast sun gives a soft glow, the midday sun a glint of its disk. Point and spot lights have a
  **Source Radius** for their highlights (a lantern's or a fire's glint on wet ground). A material's IOR sets its
  reflectance (water 2%, glass 4% as before). Screen-space reflections at High and Ultra lost their screen-door pattern on
  rough metal, their ghost columns beside thin objects and their sky wedges at the screen's sides; ambient occlusion no
  longer darkens what they found; reflection probes are 256 texels per face at High and Ultra and their bakes hold
  neither the sun's disk nor the lights' highlights; the physical sky after sunset no longer shows bands.
- **Smooth motion at any frame rate.** Games and the editor's Play and Simulate draw bodies, characters and what scripts
  move in fixed updates between their last two physics steps: the Third-Person robot running across a 4K view jittered
  by up to 29.4 pixels at 700 frames per second, now by at most 0.37 from 30 to 730. The simulation is unchanged; what you
  see is a constant step behind the game's clock (Project Settings > Physics > Timing > Interpolation turns it off). The
  same page's **Fixed Rate** (Scene, 30, 60, 120 or 240) sets every scene's fixed step for the whole project.
- **Placing content.** Drag a prefab, model or mesh from the Content Browser into the Scene view: a ghost box shows where
  it will land, snapping applies, Escape cancels, and assets the view cannot take say so at the pointer.
- **Editor.** File > Preferences > Frame Rate sets when the editor idles, how fast it redraws then and an optional cap
  while it is busy, and caps now hold on Windows (a 120 Hz cap ran 20% slow). The Performance panel's Pause (or Space)
  holds the frame breakdown still. New debug views: **Voxels**, **GI**, **Open** and **Probes** for global illumination,
  **Specular** and **Diffuse** (one kind of light alone), **Clusters** (lights per cluster), **Wireframe** and **Culled**
  (what culling left out, and why).
- **AI agents.** `asset.place` and `scene.raycast` place content where it lands; `lighting.makeAreaLight` makes a panel
  a light; `editor.screenshot` takes a picture of the whole editor window or one panel; `viewport.capture` says whether
  its picture is settled (`complete`, and `incomplete` with what it lacks), bounds every wait, and switches techniques
  for one picture (`renderer`: specular anti-aliasing, energy compensation, dithering, one bounce of global
  illumination, occlusion culling, texture streaming and more) to show what they do; `asset.types` lists the asset
  types and their importers; `editor.commands` reports which commands answer later (`mayDefer`) and wait for the asset
  scan. `StrataEditor --window-size <width>x<height>` opens a window of exactly that size, so that screenshots compare
  across machines.
- **Shaders in Slang.** Every shader is written in Slang's HLSL syntax and compiled by slangc 2026.20, still to SPIR-V
  for Vulkan and MoltenVK: the step toward other platforms' shader compilers. Pictures differ from 0.7.0's by rounding
  only. GLFW is sealed behind the window, and the engine builds without it (headless only).
- **Fixes.** Occlusion culling hid objects in plain view at sizes such as 1920x1080 and 3840x2160 (its depth pyramid
  was built a texel off where a level halves to an odd size). A program whose frame an exception ended exited with code
  0; it now exits with code 1. Script modules built with GCC or Clang (Linux, macOS) carried no debug information in
  Release and Dist, so breakpoints in scripts did not bind. Built with Apple Clang 17, a gamepad axis action read 0 when
  pushed to its negative side. A window that cannot be opened ends the program through its error message (exit code 1)
  instead of aborting. A project opened without a start scene kept the default collision layers until a setting
  changed. A capture right after `scene.save` was not settled. `lighting.bakeProbes` baked a probe that a command had just
  moved from the place it had left. A Scene view with no room under its toolbar stopped a Debug editor.

## Upgrading from 0.7.0

- **What High and Ultra draw.** Stored values are kept; these change what the presets draw:
  - The sun's shadows: High 4096 texels below 1440 output rows and 8192 from them (was 2048 and 4096), Ultra 8192, and a
    fifth cascade. At High the sun's shadow maps take 1 GiB of video memory at 3840x2160 (256 MiB at 1920x1080; 0.7.0:
    256 MiB at 3840x2160). Graphics Quality Custom with Shadow Resolution 4096, or Medium, keeps less. A light's Cascade
    Distribution now shares 1.5 m to the shadow distance (0.9 stays the default).
  - Reflection probes are 256 texels per face (68 MiB per view with probes instead of 17); Custom with **Reflection probe
    resolution** 128 keeps 0.7.0's. No probe needs baking again.
  - 64 point and spot lights cast shadows at High and 256 at Ultra (was 16): more GPU time in scenes with many shadowed
    lights; Custom with **Shadowed point and spot lights** 16 keeps 0.7.0's. **Point and spot shadow updates per frame**
    is a new row (12, 12, 24, 48).
- **Every preset.** Contact shadows are off (they shimmered while the camera moved): Graphics Quality Custom with
  Contact Shadows 12 (or 16) brings them back.
- **Shading.** The sun's highlight is its disk's size (mirrors' glints 1.8 times wider at the default 0.53 degrees;
  Angular Diameter 0 keeps a point, and hardens the shadows). The Directional Light's Shadow Softness is now **Angular
  Diameter**, with the light's own settings, active with and without shadows (still stored and scripted as
  `ShadowSoftness`). A non-metal's reflectance comes from its IOR: the default 1.5 gives 0.04 exactly as before. Point
  and spot shadows' filter noise is fixed under TAA. Shaders compiled by slangc differ from 0.7.0's pictures by rounding
  only. Asset thumbnails render once more.
- **Reflection probes baked under the atmosphere or beside shiny lit things** keep the sun's disk or the lights'
  highlights in their captures until baked again (Bake Reflection Probes, or `lighting.bakeProbes`): bake them again to
  lose those bright patches.
- **Motion is drawn between physics steps.** A script that places something from a moving body's position every frame
  (a follow camera, an effect) should read `GetDrawnPosition()` and `GetDrawnRotation()` in `OnLateUpdate`, or it seems
  to jitter against the body; a script that moves an entity far in `OnFixedUpdate` calls
  `GetTransform().ResetInterpolation()` after the move, or it is drawn sliding there over a step. Project Settings >
  Physics > Timing > Interpolation off keeps 0.7.0's drawing.
- **The editor's drops.** A prefab dropped through the Game camera lands under the pointer as the scene's camera sees it;
  a drop where nothing is under the pointer lands on the grid (y = 0, within 500 m) or in front of the camera; a prefab
  with several top-level entities moves as a whole; an asset the view cannot take shows a label at the pointer instead
  of a Console warning. Add to Scene still places under the pivot.
- **Agents' command scripts.** `viewport.capture` no longer reports `shadowFitSettled`, nor `editor.perf` and
  `viewport.capture` `renderStats.shadowFit` and each cascade's `fitted`: drop expectations of them. `input.*` durations
  in `seconds` given before play starts are checked against the step play will run (the project's Fixed Rate when it
  sets one) instead of 1/60 s. A script that needs a settled picture expects `/complete`.
- **Scripts.** The script ABI keeps version 1: 0.8.0 appends three host functions for drawn transforms (165 in all) and
  removes none, so script modules built for 0.7.0 load unchanged. New in the SDK: `Time::GetStepAlpha`,
  `TransformComponent::GetDrawnPosition`, `GetDrawnRotation` and `ResetInterpolation`. `Time::GetFixedDeltaTime` returns
  the step the scene runs, the project's Fixed Rate when it sets one.
- **Exported games** keep the runtime they were exported with: export again to get 0.8.0's.
- **Sample copies.** A copy of a sample made with 0.7.0 stays that copy: open a new copy to get 0.8.0's samples.
- **Building from source.** The glslang submodule is gone: after pulling, `Strata/vendor/glslang` (about 900 MB, which
  git leaves) may be deleted, unless you build commits from before 0.8.0. The first configure downloads Slang's release
  archive (25-64 MB) into a per-user cache (`%LOCALAPPDATA%\Strata\Toolchains`, `~/.cache/strata/toolchains`,
  `~/Library/Caches/Strata/Toolchains`) and checks its SHA-256; offline, extract the archive `CMake/StrataSlang.cmake`
  names and configure with `-DSTRATA_SLANG_ROOT=<folder>`. Engine code: `Window::GetNativeWindow` is gone
  (`GetPlatformHandle`, `GetVulkanPresentation`), `Window::Create` returns null when no window can be opened, the
  cascades' fit (`FitShadowsToView`, `ShadowFitHolds`, `ResetShadowFit`, `ShadowFitTracker`) is removed,
  `LocalShadowViewsPerFrame` moved into `RendererQualitySettings` (`Renderer.Quality` version 7),
  `RenderData::c_MaxShadowedLocalLights` is 4096 (read `RendererQualitySettings::MaxShadowedLocalLights`),
  `SceneRendererStats::LocalShadowTileSizes` holds the first 64 lights' (`LocalShadowSizeCounts` counts all), and the
  atmosphere's tables store light times `Atmosphere::c_StorageScale` (multiply by `EnvironmentParams.x`).

## What you can do with it

### Editor

- Start from the launcher: New Project (an empty project, or the Basic 3D template: a lit scene with a camera, a sun and
  the physical atmosphere, Filmic and TAA, and a ground with a collider), Open Project, Open Sample (the High Desert
  Outpost, the Third-Person Adventure, the Open World, the Physics Playground and Tetris, games an AI agent made through
  the editor's commands) and the recent projects, each with a picture of its scene; File > Preferences... opens there
  too.
- Build scenes in dockable panels: the Scene view (gizmos, snapping, a quality chip, drops with a ghost of where content
  lands, and debug views for streaming, LOD, mips, motion, shadows, light, global illumination, light clusters, wireframe
  and culling) and the Game view, Hierarchy, Inspector, Asset tab, Content Browser, Console, Problems, Activity,
  Streaming, Performance (with a pause) and Project Settings (Graphics Quality, Physics Simulation and Timing among its
  pages). Every edit can be undone; the scene plays, pauses, steps and simulates in the editor.
- Drop files on the window to import them: glTF 2.0 models (also meshopt-compressed, with clear coat and glass), PNG,
  JPEG, TGA, BMP, PSD, GIF and Radiance HDR textures, `.cube` color lookups, WAV, MP3, FLAC and Ogg Vorbis audio, and
  TrueType fonts. Imports run in the background, within a memory budget.
- The Content Browser has a folder tree, grid and list views, filters, drag and drop, and thumbnails. Deleting an asset
  that something uses is refused; a delete moves the files to the project's trash, and undo restores them.
- Prefab instances stay linked and keep their overrides: apply and revert them per property or whole, and unpack them.
- Scenes, worlds and the project file save in the background. Unsaved changes are journaled a few seconds after each
  edit and offered for recovery after a crash. File > Export Game... writes the game in the background.

### Worlds

- A world is a persistent scene plus a World asset (`.stworld`) of layers cut into cells, each cell a scene file.
  Streaming sources (or the primary camera) load the cells within their radius; the Open World sample is one.
- Edit a world in place: the edit layer decides where new content goes, pin cells on the cell map so they stay loaded,
  and save only what changed. Exported games wait for the cells of layers marked Load Before Start, then stream the
  rest.

### Rendering

- Physically based shading with energy compensation and specular anti-aliasing, clear coat and glass, reflectance from
  the IOR, lit by HDR environment maps, a procedural sky or a physical atmosphere with aerial perspective, by reflection
  probes baked in the editor and screen-space reflections, by clustered point and spot lights (up to 256 of them casting
  shadows at Ultra, highlights sized by their Source Radius), by rect lights with exact diffuse light, shaped highlights
  and soft shadows, by the directional light's stable cascaded shadows with a physical penumbra and a highlight of the
  sun's size, and, as an experiment, by global illumination traced in the world. Height fog, ambient occlusion, HDR
  with automatic exposure, bloom, the Filmic and other tone curves, color grading, a lens vignette, motion blur, depth of
  field, film grain, chromatic aberration, lens flares, FXAA, TAA or morphological anti-aliasing, and
  signed-distance-field text. Lights are stated in lux, candela, nits and lumens.
- Graphics Quality presets (Low, Medium, High, Ultra, Custom) scale shadows, shadowed lights, ambient occlusion,
  reflections, global illumination, levels of detail and motion blur; nothing renders below native resolution.
- Textures are block-compressed on import and stream their mip levels; meshes and textures load in the background within
  memory budgets. The GPU culls and draws the scene for the view and every shadow, picks levels of detail by their error
  on screen and renders relative to the camera; where a GPU cannot draw this way, the CPU culls and batches.

### Game UI

- Screen-space and world-space canvases, rects with anchors and pivots, images, text, buttons, toggles, sliders, input
  fields, layout groups and masks, navigated with the mouse, the keyboard and gamepads through the rebindable input
  actions of the map **UI**. The editor shows the UI over the scene and lays a canvas out in its 2D mode.

### Physics, audio and animation

- box3d rigid bodies with box, sphere, capsule and mesh colliders, triggers, collision events, raycasts and overlap
  queries; character controllers; named collision layers; the project's Physics Simulation settings and its Fixed Rate;
  motion drawn between the last two physics steps, smooth at any frame rate.
- miniaudio playback with spatial sources, a listener and one-shot sounds.
- glTF skins and animations import as skeletons, clips and skinned meshes, skinned on the GPU. Animation controllers
  (`.stanimator`) have states, parameters, cross-fades, events, root motion and attachment joints.

### Scripting

- Game logic is C++ classes in the project's `Scripts` folder. Scripts > New Script... starts one, Ctrl+B builds them,
  Play builds changed scripts first, and rebuilding while the game plays hot-reloads them and keeps their fields. A
  crash in a script stops play mode; the editor keeps running.
- The SDK covers entities, components, transforms (also as drawn), prefabs, input and input actions, time, physics,
  audio, animators, scene loads and sub-scenes, game UI, worlds, the game's window, the players' graphics options, save
  files, random numbers and timers. `Docs/Manual/Scripting.md` in the archive is the guide.

### Scenes and exported games

- Scenes load in the background, in the editor and in games. An exported game is a folder with the runtime renamed
  after the game, a `.stgame` manifest, the game's icon, a compressed `.stpak` asset pack and the script module. Its
  executable runs with a window or `--headless`; `--quality`, `--frames`, `--seconds`, `--screenshot`, `--resolution`,
  `--perf-log` and `--asset-budget-mb` help test it.

### AI agents

- Every editor operation is a command with a JSON Schema, the same for the UI, command scripts and agents. `StrataCLI`
  calls them from the command line, and `StrataCLI mcp` offers them to MCP clients as tools, in toolsets. An agent can
  start an editor itself, with or without a window, of an exact window size.
- Agents play the game with simulated input counted in game updates, step a paused game exactly, place content where it
  lands, see the game through viewport captures that say whether they are settled and the editor through screenshots,
  and switch rendering techniques for one capture to compare them. `Skills/` in the archive holds the guides that teach
  an agent to make a game with the editor.

### Instruments and scale

- The Streaming and Performance panels, the status pills and `asset.stats`, `physics.stats` and `editor.perf` show
  memory budgets, loads, physics, world streaming and where each frame's CPU and GPU time goes; global illumination has
  its GPU stage (`GPU.RadianceCascades`), and `editor.perf` reports shadowed lights, rect lights and moving objects.
- Measured on the Windows development machine (Intel Core i7-13700K, NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5090, Release builds) while it
  ran on temporary, slower memory, so the numbers are not comparable to 0.7.0's: at 3840x2160 and High, global
  illumination's own stage takes about 4.2 ms of GPU time; eight 2 x 1 m rect lights light a room in 1.27 ms, and four
  of them casting shadows take 8.65 ms, 7.3 of it their shadow masks; the fifth sun cascade costs 0.32 ms where every
  cascade is drawn again. With 1,000 casters moving under 256 shadowed point lights at Ultra, choosing the shadow views to
  draw again takes 0.31 ms of a frame on the main thread.

## Requirements

Every platform needs a GPU with a Vulkan 1.2 driver to show the editor and play games. Started without one, the editor
(and a game) shows a message that says so, what to install and where its log is. Without a GPU the editor still runs
headless (`StrataEditor --no-gpu`, for automation and exports) and games run with `--headless`. The GPU must support
cube map arrays (Vulkan's `imageCubeArray`), as in 0.7.0; 0.8.0 asks for nothing more.

- **Windows 10 or 11, x64.** The Vulkan runtime comes with the graphics drivers of NVIDIA, AMD and Intel GPUs; update
  the driver if the editor reports that Vulkan is not available. Nothing else is needed to run Strata.
- **Linux x64: Ubuntu 24.04 or newer**, or another distribution with glibc 2.39 and the C++ library of GCC 14. The
  editor needs the Vulkan loader, a Vulkan driver, GTK 3 and the X11 or Wayland libraries (NVIDIA GPUs get Vulkan from
  NVIDIA's driver); exported games need the same without GTK. On Ubuntu:

  ```sh
  sudo apt install libvulkan1 mesa-vulkan-drivers libgtk-3-0t64 libx11-6 libxrandr2 libxinerama1 \
    libxcursor1 libxi6 libxkbcommon0 libwayland-client0
  ```

- **macOS 14 or newer, Apple silicon.** Vulkan runs on Metal through MoltenVK, which the archive does not include:
  install the Vulkan SDK from https://vulkan.lunarg.com/sdk/home#mac (choose "System Global Installation"), or run
  `brew install molten-vk vulkan-loader`.

**Building scripts** needs a C++ compiler and CMake 3.25 or later. The editor looks for them at the first script build
and says exactly what is missing:

- Windows: Visual Studio 2022 (17.10 or later) or 2026 with the "Desktop development with C++" workload, or the Build
  Tools for Visual Studio with the C++ build tools. Its C++ CMake tools (installed with the workload by default) provide
  CMake; a CMake from https://cmake.org/download/ works too.
- Linux: `sudo apt install g++ cmake ninja-build`.
- macOS: `xcode-select --install` (the Command Line Tools), and `brew install cmake ninja` or CMake.app from
  https://cmake.org/download/.

The samples' scripts are built the same way, so opening a sample and pressing Play needs them too.

## First start

The executables are not signed, so the system asks before it runs them the first time.

- **Windows:** before unpacking, right-click the downloaded `.zip`, choose Properties and tick **Unblock**, so the
  unpacked files (and the games you export, which copy the runtime) are not marked as downloaded. Otherwise SmartScreen
  shows "Windows protected your PC" when you start the editor: choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
- **macOS:** remove the quarantine mark from the unpacked folder once, in Terminal, then start `./StrataEditor` in it:
  `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine ~/Downloads/Strata-0.8.0-macos-arm64` (use the folder's real path).
- **Linux:** nothing to do; start `./StrataEditor` in the folder.

## Known issues

### Platforms

- **Wave 8 was developed and tested on Windows with an NVIDIA GPU** (RTX 5090, driver 621.1), where every GPU test runs
  under the Vulkan validation layer. This release changes every shader. On the Mac, CI converted every shader to Metal
  and compiled it, but no GPU test ran through MoltenVK; on Mesa's lavapipe (a CPU driver, tried on CI) 529 of 553 GPU
  tests pass with the new shaders as with the old, and the same 24 fail with both; AMD and Intel GPUs and Linux drivers
  have not run them: rendering problems there are especially worth reporting (see
  [Reporting problems](#reporting-problems)). CI builds every platform and runs the tests that need no GPU. The editor
  has not been looked at on a Windows display at 150% since 0.3.0.
- **macOS:** the editor, its rendering, the samples and exported games ran on one Mac (Apple M2 Max, macOS 26, MoltenVK
  1.4) up to 0.6.0; other Macs and macOS 14 and 15 have not been tried. MoltenVK and the Vulkan loader must be installed
  separately (see [Requirements](#requirements)). Metal has no multi-draw, so scenes of thousands of different meshes
  cost more there. An exported game is not an app bundle: the Finder shows no icon or version for it.
- **Linux:** the windowed editor has had less use on Linux than on the other platforms. The export writes the game's
  desktop entry but does not install it, and it names the folder's absolute paths, so export again after moving the
  folder.
- **Windows:** script builds in the system's temporary folder or under a short (8.3) path list MSBuild's warnings
  MSB8029 and MSB8012 in the Problems panel. The editor and exported games are windowed programs, which PowerShell does
  not wait for: pipe their output to wait for one and get its exit code, e.g. `.\StrataEditor --version | Write-Output`.
  If a windowed editor or game runs far above the display's rate with vsync on, the graphics driver is overriding vsync:
  set NVIDIA Control Panel's "Vertical sync" (Manage 3D settings) to "Use the 3D application setting" (borderless and
  fullscreen windows were not measured).
- The executables are not signed (see [First start](#first-start)).
- The performance numbers of this release were measured while the development machine ran on temporary, slower memory.

### Global illumination (experimental)

Global illumination is off unless a scene's Post Process turns it on, and off in every sample. Measured at High against
a one-bounce reference and in the look-dev scenes (a sunlit room, a yard, a Cornell box) at 3840x2160:

- **Light.** In the sunlit room the east wall high and the ceiling's east side get 0.63 and 0.49 of their bounce (8 of 10
  wall and ceiling points within 30%, mean error 20%), one floor-wall junction misses, and two points behind the camera
  2 m up get 0.62-0.66. Low is too dark (0.2-0.5 of the reference) and bounces once: with every bounce its Cornell box
  would be about 1.8 times as bright as High's. Open ground under the sky reads 3.2% off at 6 m; a white sphere under a
  uniform sky reads 2% dark at its center and 3.5% at its rim, up to 10% where a probe lies within a voxel of it (3% is
  the goal).
- **Cost.** About 4.2 ms of GPU time at 3840x2160 and High (`GPU.RadianceCascades`; 1-2 ms was aimed at), and the
  forward pass's gather adds 0.3-0.8 ms plus 0.10-0.12 ms to `GPU.Opaque`. The stage read about 4.4 ms in some runs and
  6.5 ms in others of one build, and `GPU.Opaque` has two states 0.3-0.4 ms apart that change from one capture to the
  next; the causes are not established, so one reading can be 2 ms high: compare builds by interleaved runs and by the
  frame's busy time.
- **Thin gaps and recesses.** A surface that the probes around it see little of gets little indirect light, darker than
  without global illumination: gaps, recesses and ledges thinner than about two voxels (20 cm at High, 40 cm at Low) and
  the bases of walls beside bright patches can go nearly black (a gap no probe sees keeps a few percent of the light at
  its opening). This shows on ThirdPerson's ground ledges and fence rails, in a strip along the foot of large boxes, as a
  dark band about 0.3 m high where a floor meets the wall under a sunlit window, and in ThirdPerson's ground folds, which
  are about half as bright as before batch 2 (2,451 pixels of the 4K picture fell under half of their luma). With the
  sun high, the bounce on a window wall ends in sharp vertical edges either side of the patch of sunlight.
- **The edges of thin gaps.** The ground right at a thin gap's edge beyond about 5.4 m from the camera (under a covered
  car) reads about twice as bright as from nearer, and ends in a hard line 0.25 m under the object's edge (from 0.94 to
  0.076 of the open ground's light from one pixel to the next). Where the finer voxels' window ends, the light of a gap
  that crosses it steps (by up to four times far inside the gap; measured under the car, 1 to 11 of 255 in the GI view).
  The gaps' light can end in a crisp edge: on one of ThirdPerson's ground folds, a patch about 25 pixels across steps by
  14-16 of 255 in the GI view and 3 in the lit picture.
- **Angled walls** glow softly around a patch of sunlight on them: on a panel turned 30 degrees from the world's axes,
  the wall at the patch reads 2.2 to 2.7 times its shadowed part's indirect light.
- **Range.** Its light ends at the probe field's edge around the camera (High: full within 7.5 m horizontally and 5.5 m
  up and down; Low: 7 m and 2.8 m; faded out over 2 m): outdoors the edge of that bubble can show.
- **The Cornell box.** The probes are 0.5 m apart at High (1 m at Low), and the light falls faster than it should where
  they cannot resolve it. A row of small hard-edged black notches remains where the sphere meets the floor (a gap of 6-15
  cm that no probe sees), with soft lighter streaks beside it; the floor between the tall box and the red wall falls to
  0.22 of its neighbour's light 0.1 m away (half is the goal); a soft dark patch, 0.6 m by 0.2 m, lies on the floor along
  the tall box's left face (under half of its light before batch 2, in 2,555 pixels), and the floor 0.3 to 0.6 m from the
  sphere's contact is darker too (mean green 24 of 255 against 34). At Low, the walls and the floor beside the box and the
  sphere show steps and black patches and the ceiling darkens along its edges. In the sunlit room the window wall keeps
  dark vertical bands either side of the window, a glow up to 1.8 times too bright near its edges and a dark band along
  its foot.
- **Settling.** With every bounce, rooms take frames to fill after a cut, by their brightest surface (the sunlit room 13
  frames, the Cornell box 18, a closed white box 39): a game's cut shows the light rising. Captures wait for it.
- **Repeats.** Two captures of one still shot are not always equal, with or without global illumination (a polished
  metal shot without anti-aliasing differs in 3,077 pixels; a steel sheet with TAA at Medium in 5% of the frame), though
  consecutive frames of a still camera are. On the development machine's driver the light field's bits also do not
  always repeat: a still camera's test failed in 1 of 3 runs at times, and two renderers of equal voxels differed in one
  byte of 4.4 million in one run of seven. Image comparisons need a tolerance beyond a shot's own repeats.
- **Moving objects.** While an object moves, each of its parts is taken as a box of its size with soft edges, so a
  ball, a barrel or a hollow object shades and bounces light as its box would until it rests; a quarter of a second
  after it stops its exact shape takes over, and the light beside it changes by what the two differ by: up to 26% at
  0.3 m from a 0.5 m box under an open sky. In a closed room lit by its bounces the change is larger: in the Cornell box a
  stopping box shows 59% to 72% of its shade and tint on the objects beside it and 25% on the floor in that frame, the
  light then first moves away from where it ends (by up to 14.8% of a point's light), and one frame steps the floor
  beside it by 27.6%. Under an open sky single frames step a point's light by up to 4.3% (10.1% once) as an object starts
  and stops. An object that moves away fast leaves its old shade fading for a quarter of a second.
- **Before a moving object.** In a room lit by its bounces, the floor right before a moving box's leading face flickers
  as the face passes a row of probes (0.5 m apart at High): by about a tenth of its light, second differences of 19.1%
  where 2% is the goal (beside the object's way the goal holds).
- **Limits.** At most 64 moving parts and 512 bricks of voxels (256 at Low) move at once: what is past them is left out
  of the bounce light until it rests. Objects thinner than a voxel are dark while they move. Animated characters
  receive the bounce light without blocking or bouncing it.
- **With rect lights,** a scene with global illumination counts a light surface's light twice (once from its rect
  light, once through the GI): in the Cornell box with its panel made a light, the floor's center reads 162 nits against
  43.8 from the light alone. Look at such scenes with global illumination off until area lights reach it.

### Rect lights

- **Highlights** are the published fit's, less exact than GGX at grazing views of middling roughness: median and 95th
  percentile of the relative error 0.022 and 0.52 at roughness 0.25, 0.072 and 0.81 at 0.5, 0.053 and 0.68 at 0.75
  (0.0063 and 0.105, 0.024 and 0.332, 0.026 and 0.48 within 53.6 degrees of the normal). Seen 87 degrees from the
  normal, faces in the mirror direction get 1.1 to 1.4 times GGX's light at roughness 0.25 and 1.6 to 2.4 times at 0.5;
  a metal floor of roughness 0.3 holds 0.934 of GGX's light in its highlight, and a white metal of roughness 0.3 to 0.6
  under a light filling the sky reflects 0.912 to 0.918 of it at 78 degrees.
- **Shadows.** Penumbrae wider than an eighth of the light's shadow tile come out narrower than real ones (a blocker
  0.3 m under a 2 m light: 16% of the true width); a contact shadow is 2.0 cm wide where the true one is 1.2 cm; in the
  Cornell box 1.3% of the floor's pixels are more than 0.1 off the true visibility, its penumbrae within 0.129, some light
  passes the tall box's corner at its foot (up to 0.45 of it, on 9 of 30,142 pixels), and the red wall in that box's
  shadow reads up to 0.18 too dark at the shadow's outer edge and 0.08 too light inside. A light whose shadow tile halves
  as the camera moves away draws its penumbra 9% narrower. Where a single-sided plane lies in front of a closed mesh the
  space between them counts as solid, and a double-sided closed mesh is known only by its front. A side facing away from
  the light's center finds only some blockers (0.12 off the true visibility on average, 0.29 at worst), and a steep side
  with a blocker between it and the light's center reads fully shadowed (0.26 too dark on average). A very large rect
  light (100 x 100 m) drew ragged notches along a sphere's terminator.
- **Cost.** Four rect lights casting shadows over a whole 4K view at High take 7.3 ms of GPU time for their masks (about
  1.7 ms each, where 0.2 ms was estimated); 45% of the fully lit pixels still run the filter's comparisons. At most four
  rect lights cast shadows at once; a fifth waits.
- **Shaders.** A scene with a rect light, even an unlit one out of sight, draws every surface with the forward shaders
  that shade rect lights (2.7% more opaque pass time in the showcase at Medium). Adding the first rect light to a running
  scene or removing the last (a spawned prefab, a streamed cell) switches them, and that frame compiles the forward
  pipelines it draws on the main thread: 6.3-7.1 ms for one pipeline of a small scene, more for scenes of more kinds of
  surfaces (not measured).

### Shadows

- **Sun shadows.** Medium (2048 texels) draws them as 0.6.0 did: at 3840x2160 a texel spans about 2.2 screen pixels at
  each split, coarser than 0.7.0's Medium. High and Ultra are finer than 0.7.0's in most views (a median of 0.77 times
  0.7.0's screen pixels per texel over the look-dev shots) but coarser where 0.7.0 fitted its cascades to a few close or
  crowded surfaces (medians of pixels per texel, 0.7.0 against 0.8.0: the showcase's shot 5 past a door post 0.3 m away
  0.88 against 1.48; its macro shot within 0.5 m 1.24 against 8.35, without a shadow edge in it; the look-dev sun room
  from behind 0.88 against 1.61; editor views from high above 0.31 and 0.57 against 0.96, still under a pixel).
- **Contact shadows are off.** A thin object's shadow near the camera (a 5 cm plate on the floor) stays solid at High,
  but its edge shows the shadow map's texel steps; Graphics Quality Custom with Contact Shadows 12 smooths it, at the
  cost of shimmer in motion. Contact shadows can leave a 1-pixel fringe beside thin foliage.
- **Many shadowed lamps.** At High and Ultra, with many lamps in view, the nearest lamps' shadows are softer than 0.7.0's
  and Medium's, as the extra lights share the atlas: in a town of lanterns at 3840x2160 and High, the 8 nearest get two
  512-texel and six 256-texel tiles (512 each in 0.7.0) beside 47 lamps at 128, and a crate's shadow edge widens from 2-3
  to 5 pixels; at Ultra with 143 lamps shadowed the nearest lamp's tiles fall from 2048 to 1024 texels (its edge from 3
  to 4-6 pixels). Custom with **Shadowed point and spot lights** 16 gives High Medium's tiles. At Medium the eighth or
  ninth lamp gets 256 texels instead of 512.
- **Point and spot shadow edges under TAA** keep the filter's fixed pattern, which 0.7.0's turning noise let the history
  average away (a converged penumbra keeps 0.71 of one frame's roughness under Original and 0.55 under Sharp, against
  0.042 and 0.047 in 0.7.0): a stipple on soft edges seen at three times a capture's size. Without anti-aliasing and with
  FXAA they are unchanged. Their softness is a filter radius, not the lamp's size (Source Radius sizes only highlights).
- Beyond 4,096 shadow casters per shadowed light within the lights' ranges (1,048,576 in all at Ultra), the shadow
  views' tracking of casters starts over, and any caster's change then draws every view again until the tracking holds
  them all. A camera cut that reorders many shadowed lights orders them in quadratic time in that frame:
  0.02-0.04 ms at Ultra's 256 lights, 3.7-7.0 ms at Custom's ceiling of 3,584 spot lights.

### Reflections and highlights

- In motion, screen-space reflections at High and Ultra still add a fine crawl to rough metal and wet ground (in the
  reflections of thin posts, shiny balls and edges): a third to a half less than 0.7.0's without TAA, up to a third
  less on metal with TAA and none less on wet ground with TAA.
- Thin posts far behind a reflection (12 cm at 30 m) fall between the march's far steps: a rough metal sheet shows the
  wall behind them. At Ultra, shiny balls reflected in rough metal keep a fine grain in still pictures (noise 1.05-1.08%
  against the 1% aimed at; High 0.95-0.96%).
- An object in front of a mirror is reflected only where the camera also sees it (often a crescent), with the probes or
  the sky where its back would be. Where a mirror meets a ball's side at a slant, the march misses part of it in fine
  stripes (42% of those pixels hit at High, 65% at Ultra), which the probes fill.
- With TAA, a highlight that slides over a surface as the camera moves pumps by 4.5-9.5% from frame to frame (a Source
  Radius roughly halves it on lanterns); without TAA lanterns' glints on wet ground pump by 1.4% as points and 0.3%
  sized, with FXAA by 4.2% and 1.3%.
- Screen-space reflections show only on surfaces smoother than **Screen Space Reflections Max Roughness**, reflect only
  what is on screen, and transparent surfaces get none; Medium's are traced in a checkerboard that needs TAA. Reflection
  probes are baked in the editor, never while the game runs, and at most 16 probes in view light a scene.

### Rendering

- **Motion drawn between physics steps.** While a game runs on the clock, world-space game UI, editor gizmos, picking,
  reflection probes, fog and post-process volumes, the sky and the sun, and attachments placed outside their animator's
  hierarchy use the simulated poses, up to one step off what is drawn (exact while paused or stepped). A character that
  rides something a script moves in `OnUpdate` is drawn a step behind it (5 cm at 3 m/s and 60 steps per second): move
  carriers in `OnFixedUpdate` or as kinematic bodies. A frame that runs a fixed step with 100,000 moving roots takes
  5.5 ms against 2.6 ms without interpolation (a real scene in the editor paid about 0.05 ms), and moving shadow casters
  redraw their shadows every frame (about 2,000 rolling balls in the Physics Playground: 356 instead of 501 frames per
  second in the Game view); Interpolation off removes both costs.
- **Motion blur is off in the template and every sample**, and in new Post Process components: it smeared some moving
  edges too wide and others too narrow. Turn it on per scene with **Motion Blur** on the Post Process.
- **FXAA** keeps 0.87 of the texture detail of an image without anti-aliasing, and morphological anti-aliasing 0.88;
  TAA Sharp keeps 0.92 and stays the 3D default. TAA keeps a few softer frames where a moving object uncovers what it
  hid, and would soften 2D games and pixel art: keep FXAA there.
- **Glass** shows only what was drawn before it, gets no ambient occlusion and casts no shadow, and its thickness is one
  value per material. Height fog is not volumetric (no light shafts), and the atmosphere is computed for a viewer near
  the ground.

### Project settings and input

- Opening a scene clears the undo history, the project settings' steps with it; each change was saved in the project
  file at once, so nothing is lost.
- Only the Third-Person Adventure and the High Desert Outpost's walk read their controls as input actions, and no sample
  has a menu for rebinding yet. Input actions have no processors, modifiers or chords; mouse motion and the scroll wheel
  are not action sources; all players share one set of actions; and the names of keys a game shows do not follow the
  player's keyboard layout.
- Gamepads are still read while an exported game is in the background. Scripts cannot read the game's title or version.
  Gravity remains a setting of each scene; at a Fixed Rate of 30 contacts are softer.

### Samples

- The samples take about 113 MB of the unpacked archive (80 MB of it the High Desert Outpost's, 17 MB the Open
  World's), and a copy imports its models and textures the first time it opens.
- **High Desert Outpost:** no grass, no people and no sound. Its probes hold the sky alone, and global illumination
  stays off in it, so what the sun does not reach is dark: at dusk 22.6% of shot 6's pixels are under 8 of 255 (the
  lean-to's mean 11.7), and the hut's roof underside at golden hour reads a mean of 56.5. The photo mode's day ends at
  dusk (19.0 h), with no night after it, and its reflections snap to the nearest of the three baked times of day. At
  Medium the puddles reflect only the probes with FXAA or without anti-aliasing.
- The Third-Person robot's face stays neutral (the importer skips morph targets). The Third-Person Adventure has no
  music and the Open World no sound. The Open World loads no distant vegetation, and its day keeps the dim sun and moon
  it had.

### Editor

- An idle editor runs at 30 frames per second by default (10 without focus; File > Preferences > Frame Rate), so it
  answers the first input or automation request after a pause on its next frame.
- The Inspector reads every selected entity's values each frame: selecting 100,000 entities adds about 8 ms to a frame.
- Opening a scene of a million entities can still hold a frame for 1.2-6.5 ms, about every other time.
- There is no prefab edit mode: change a prefab through an instance and apply it. Prefabs and the Asset tab's editors
  still save in the frame, not in the background as scenes do. The Scene view's drop ghost is a box, not the mesh.
- At a fixed resolution the Game view gives the game the editor's UI scale as its display's content scale.

### Worlds and game UI

- Keep worlds within 20 km of the origin. Distant cells are not drawn at all (there are no simplified far versions yet).
- Moving bodies belong to the cell they loaded with and unload with it; references between entities of different cells
  resolve only while both cells are loaded.
- World-space canvases are drawn unlit, without shadows, after post-processing, and the game's clicks on them do not
  check for scene geometry in front.

### Scripts and engine

- Creating or removing many bodies at once takes longer than it did on Jolt (0.4.0): 10,000 bodies take about 19 ms on
  the development machine, 10 of them box3d's own. Spawn in smaller groups over several frames where that matters.
- A thousand walking characters take about 0.9 ms a physics step, and a few falling bodies beside tens of thousands of
  sleeping ones make box3d's own step several times slower.
- Triggers do not see triangle-mesh colliders, box3d holds at most 128 physics worlds at once, and its mover, on which
  the character controllers are built, is marked experimental there.
- On Linux and macOS a script crash names the function and its offset in the module, not the file and line. Scripts
  are native code, so an endless loop in a script blocks the editor or the game.
- HDR textures are stored uncompressed (RGBA16F) until a BC6H encoder exists, and packs store small assets one by one.
- Builds from source can leave out physics, audio, animation and the window (GLFW), but not yet scripting.

### Not in this release

Strata never renders below native resolution: there will be no render scaling below 1, dynamic resolution or temporal
upscaling. Planned for later waves: global illumination's next batch (area lights in it, its gate), disk lights,
volumetric fog and light shafts, a mesh ghost and live material previews for drops, blended material layers with
wetness and supersampled stills; an animation graph (based on Esoterica's architecture) with its editor; distant
shadows for open worlds, reflection probes captured while the game runs and MSAA; crash reports in shipped games,
exports per platform with a macOS app bundle, and signed exports; input processors, chords and local multiplayer; a
manual in the Help menu, playable templates, particles, trails and decals, simplified far versions of world cells
(HLOD), terrain with sculpting and painting, worlds beyond 20 km, OBJ and FBX import, morph targets, physics joints,
ragdolls and vehicles, an audio mixer, localization, and a prefab edit mode.

## Reporting problems

Report problems at https://github.com/2042Third/GameEngine/issues with:

- the version: Help > About Strata, or `StrataEditor --version`;
- your platform and GPU (Help > About Strata shows the GPU and the driver);
- what you did, step by step, and what happened;
- the log: `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Strata\Logs\StrataEditor.log` on Windows, `~/.local/share/Strata/Logs` on Linux and
  `~/Library/Application Support/Strata/Logs` on macOS. Exported games log to `Logs/Game.log` in a folder named after
  the game, beside the `Strata` folder.
