# Strata 0.6.0

Strata is a 3D game engine written in C++20, with an editor that people and AI agents drive through the same commands,
game logic written as C++ scripts that the editor builds, and exported games that run in StrataRuntime. 0.6.0 holds
what development wave 6 delivered, completed on 2026-10-23, on top of everything in [0.5.0][notes-0.5.0] (wave 5),
[0.4.0][notes-0.4.0] (wave 4) and [0.3.0][notes-0.3.0] (waves 1 to 3). Versions follow the waves (0.7.0 will be wave 7),
and patch versions carry fixes.

connecting an AI agent.

format 0.5.0 cannot open, and imports their textures and models once more in the background (see
[Upgrading from 0.5.0](#upgrading-from-050)). Scripts build against the same SDK, which only gained functions; export
games again to get 0.6.0's runtime and the game's title and icon.

## What's new in 0.6.0

Wave 6 took the two pieces of work the audits after wave 4 ranked first: the quality of the rendering, and settings for
the whole project (input actions, collision layers and the game's identity among them).

- **Temporal anti-aliasing (TAA).** The Post Process component's **Anti Aliasing Method** now chooses FXAA or TAA. TAA
  moves each frame's samples by a fraction of a pixel and blends the frame with the frames before it, fetched where each
  surface was (motion vectors of the camera, of moving entities and of animated characters): edges stay smooth and
  steady in motion, thin details stop flickering, and the noise of soft shadows and ambient occlusion averages away. Its
  **TAA Preset** is Sharp (the default: tighter history clipping, slow pans kept crisper, no ghosts where a moving
  object uncovers the background or behind moving transparent surfaces, and a light sharpening of the displayed image
  only), Original (the first TAA, softer and steadier) or Custom, which opens every tunable. New projects from the Basic
  3D template and the three 3D samples use TAA Sharp; FXAA stays the component's default, and the right choice for 2D
  and pixel art. The selection outline and the editor's overlays stay steady under TAA, viewport captures and the
  project's picture wait until the image settled, and the viewport's **Motion** debug view shows the motion vectors.
  `Docs/Manual/AntiAliasing.md` explains how many frames TAA keeps, when it trails and what each tunable does.
- **Shadows of point and spot lights.** Turn on **Cast Shadows** on a point or spot light: a spot light shadows its
  cone, a point light every direction. Up to 16 lights in view cast shadows at a time, the most relevant ones, and a
  light's shadow is drawn again only when something in its range moves or changes, so a still lantern over a still room
  costs nothing after its first frame. **Shadow Filter Radius**, **Shadow Bias** and **Shadow Normal Bias** tune the
  edges.
- **Seamless shadow cascades.** The sun's shadow cascades blend into each other (**Cascade Blend** on the Directional
  Light), so no seam shows where one cascade hands over to the next, and shadows fade out toward **Shadow Distance**
  instead of ending there.
- **Light units and real daylight.** Lights use the units of real light: the directional light lux, point and spot
  lights candela, the sky and emission nits. The Inspector's tooltips name each unit, and `Docs/Manual/Lighting.md`
  gives reference values with the exposure that suits them. Nothing is rescaled, so scenes keep their values. The
  renderer now scales what it stores by a power of two that follows the exposure, so a real 100,000 lux sun, its
  highlights and the sun's own disk keep their detail, and automatic exposure reaches EV 16 by default.
- **A physical atmosphere and height fog.** The Sky Light's new **Atmosphere** source computes Earth's sky from the
  directional light: deep blue at noon, orange and red around a low sun and dark once it has set, with the sunlight
  dimmed and reddened on its way through the air, all following the light as it turns. **Height Fog** (**Add Height
  Fog** in the Inspector's Sky section) is densest at a base height and thins upward; it can take the sky's light, so it
  meets the horizon without a seam, and glow toward the sun.
- **Reflection probes.** A **Reflection Probe** (Add Component, Rendering) gives shiny surfaces inside its box a picture
  of what surrounds them, and their ambient light from it. **Bake** in its Inspector section (or **Bake Reflection
  Probes** in the Sky section, for all of them) renders the scene around each probe into an HDR texture in
  `Assets/Lighting/<scene>/`, in one step you can undo; baking again replaces the earlier picture and adds light that
  bounced twice.
- **Screen-space reflections.** **Screen Space Reflections** on the Post Process (with TAA) add what the screen shows to
  smooth surfaces: a polished floor, a pond or a metal plate reflects the characters and things beside it, and the
  reflections move with them.
- **The sun of an HDR sky.** **Extract Sun** in the Sky section makes the scene's directional light the sun an
  environment map shows, in its direction, color and brightness, so that it casts shadows, and turns on the map's new
  **Remove Sun** import setting, which paints the sun's disk out of the sky so that it does not light the scene twice.
- **Clear coat and glass.** Materials gained a clear coat (a glossy layer over the base, with its own roughness, maps
  and normal map) and transmission: glass that shows what lies behind it, refracted by its **IOR** and **Thickness** and
  tinted by its volume (**Attenuation Color** and **Attenuation Distance**). glTF models bring them in through
  `KHR_materials_clearcoat`, `KHR_materials_transmission`, `KHR_materials_volume` and `KHR_materials_ior`.
- **The samples show them.** The Open World has the atmosphere and its sunsets, height fog over the island, a lantern
  that is always lit and campfires at night that cast shadows. The Third-Person Adventure's sun is extracted from its
  sky, two lanterns cast shadows under the trees, four reflection probes along the course make the robot's metal and
  the coins reflect the islands around them, and it reads its controls as input actions. The Physics Playground's
  plastic balls have a clear coat, and its ball pit has walls of glass. All three use TAA Sharp.
- **Project Settings and Preferences.** **File > Project Settings...** opens a panel of pages for the settings of the
  game, kept in the project file and carried into exported games: **Game** (the start and loading scenes and the game's
  identity), **Export** (what an export that packs only what the scenes use keeps anyway), **Collision Layers**,
  **Input Actions**, and a page for every other section the engine or a game registers. A change applies at once (also
  to a game playing in the editor), is saved in the background and is a step of the undo history that leaves the scene
  saved. **File > Preferences...**, also from the launcher, holds your own settings: the external code editor and the
  size of the editor's interface. `Docs/Manual/Settings.md` describes both.
- **Collision layers for the whole project.** The layers' names and which layers collide are one table for every scene,
  prefab, world cell and exported game, edited on the Collision Layers page. Scripts still change which layers collide
  while a scene plays, for that scene (`Physics::SetLayersCollide`).
- **Input actions with rebinding.** A project defines maps of named actions (a Button, an Axis from -1 to 1 or a
  Vector2) bound to keys, mouse buttons, gamepad buttons, axes and sticks, and composites of two or four keys such as
  WASD, on the **Input Actions** page, where a button binds the next control you press. Scripts read actions by name
  instead of keys (`Input::IsActionPressed("Jump")`, `Input::GetActionValue("Gameplay/Move")`), turn maps on and off,
  and let players rebind them: a script can capture the next control a player presses, and the players' bindings are
  kept with the game's save files. The game UI navigates with the built-in map **UI** (Submit, Cancel and the four
  directions), rebindable like any other. `Docs/Manual/Scripting.md` shows the API.
- **The game's identity.** The Game page sets the game's **Title**, **Version**, **Company**, **Copyright** and
  **Icon** (a square texture of the project, at least 256 pixels). Exported games show the title in their window bar
  and log and the icon as their window's. On Windows the export writes the icon, title, version, company and copyright
  into the game's executable, where Explorer shows them; on macOS the game shows its icon in the Dock while it runs; on
  Linux the export writes `<Game>.desktop`, which lists the game among your applications once copied into
  `~/.local/share/applications`. Changing the title or the company never moves the players' saves and logs. Every
  sample now has a title, a version and an icon.
- **Smaller changes and fixes.** Agents get the MCP toolset `settings` (project settings, collision layers, input
  actions, the game's identity and preferences), `input.action` (taps, presses or releases an action whatever its
  bindings), `lighting.bakeProbes` and `lighting.extractSun`, `play.start` with `paused` (the game starts paused before
  its first fixed step) and `viewport.capture` with `live`, `atFixedStep` and `frames` (what a view showed as the game
  played, at an exact step). Fixed: pushing the Third-Person Adventure's left stick forward walked backward, and pushing
  its right stick up looked down; meshes finished during a wait for loads could fail when the geometry pool had to grow,
  which left some of the Open World's terrain tiles undrawn in scripted starts on a busy machine (0.5.0 did it too);
  and `UI::IsActionPressed` kept reporting the last press while no canvas existed.

## Upgrading from 0.5.0

- **Projects move to a new project file.** The first time 0.6.0 opens a project saved by 0.5.0, it reads its scenes'
  collision layers in the background before the start scene opens and makes them the project's: each layer's name
  comes from the start scene first, a pair of layers any scene kept apart stays apart, and where scenes disagree the log
  says how. It then saves the project file as version 3, which **0.5.0 cannot open**: commit the project, or keep a
  copy, if you still need 0.5.0. Scene files stay as they are until you save them, which drops their own layers. A
  scene whose own layers differ from the project's plays by the project's, and the editor says so once per scene and
  session.
- **Collision layers in commands.** `scene.setSettings` no longer sets collision layers: it refuses them and names
  `project.setCollisionLayers`, which command scripts and agents use instead. The Inspector's scene settings link to the
  Collision Layers page.
- **Preferences.** The first time 0.6.0 starts, it copies the external code editor from 0.5.0's `EditorSettings.json`
  into its own `Preferences.json`; from then on each version keeps its own, so a 0.5.0 installed beside it keeps
  working as it did.
- **One more import.** Every texture now has the **Remove Sun** import setting, and the glTF importer reads clear coat
  and glass, so the first time 0.6.0 opens a project, each texture imported on its own and each glTF model imports once
  more, in the background, while the editor stays usable.
- **How 0.5.0's scenes look.** They render as before, except:
  - Their shadow cascades blend too (**Cascade Blend** 0.1, the default, also for scenes saved before it): the seams
    between cascades disappear, and shadows fade out toward **Shadow Distance** instead of ending at it. Set Cascade
    Blend to 0 for 0.5.0's shadows.
  - **Auto Exposure Max EV** defaults to 16 instead of 12. Scenes that 0.5.0 saved store 12 and keep it; a Post Process
    added now, and the Basic 3D template's, get 16. A scene without a Post Process changes only where its average
    brightness exceeds 4,096 nits (2^12), as in real daylight.
  - Their anti-aliasing stays what they chose: choose TAA on their Post Process to use it. Their lights keep their
    values.
- **Devices.** 0.6.0 needs a GPU with cube map arrays (see [Requirements](#requirements)), which every desktop GPU with
  a Vulkan 1.2 driver has.
- **Scripts.** The script ABI keeps version 1: 0.6.0 adds thirteen host functions for input actions (155 in all) and
  removes none, so script modules built for 0.5.0 load unchanged.
- **Exported games** keep the runtime they were exported with: export again to get 0.6.0's runtime, the project
  settings (the manifest's version 5) and the game's identity.
- **Sample copies.** A copy of a sample made with 0.5.0 stays that copy (its project is migrated like any other): open
  a new copy to get 0.6.0's lighting, TAA, input actions and the Third-Person Adventure's fixed sticks.

## What you can do with it

### Editor

- Start from the launcher: New Project (an empty project, or the Basic 3D template: a lit scene with a camera, a sun, a
  procedural sky, TAA and a ground with a collider), Open Project, Open Sample (the Third-Person Adventure, the Open
  World, the Physics Playground and Tetris, games an AI agent made through the editor's commands) and the recent
  projects, each with a picture of its scene; File > Preferences... opens there too.
- Build scenes in dockable panels: the Scene view (gizmos, snapping, the Streaming, LOD, Mip and Motion debug views,
  cell outlines in worlds) and the Game view, Hierarchy (multi-selection, drag and drop, rename in place, filters, UI
  presets, a world's layers and cells), Inspector (edits every selected entity at once, marks prefab overrides, shows
  the scene's settings with its sky, fog, sun extraction and probe bakes), Asset tab (materials, import settings,
  previews, worlds' layers), Content Browser, Console, Problems, Activity, Streaming (with the world's cell map and
  physics), Performance and Project Settings. Every edit can be undone; the scene plays, pauses, steps and simulates in
  the editor.
- Drop files on the window to import them: glTF 2.0 models (also meshopt-compressed, with clear coat and glass), PNG,
  JPEG, TGA, BMP, PSD, GIF and Radiance HDR textures, WAV, MP3, FLAC and Ogg Vorbis audio, and TrueType fonts. Imports
  run in the background, within a memory budget.
- The Content Browser has a folder tree, grid and list views, filters, drag and drop, and thumbnails of textures,
  materials, meshes, models and prefabs. Deleting an asset that something uses (a scene, or a project setting: the start
  scene or the game's icon, for example) is refused; a delete moves the files to the project's trash, and undo restores
  them. Files a save is still writing cannot be moved or deleted until it ends.
- New content rests on the surface under the view's pivot or the cursor, and End drops the selection to the ground.
  Prefab instances stay linked and keep their overrides: apply and revert them per property or whole, and unpack them;
  a prefab's changes reach every instance except where it overrides them.
- Scenes, worlds and the project file save in the background. Unsaved changes are journaled a few seconds after each
  edit and offered for recovery after a crash. The UI asks before it replaces a scene with unsaved changes or releases
  a project whose settings could not be written, commands refuse unless forced, and what is discarded stays
  recoverable.
- File > Export Game... writes the game into a folder of its own in the background, while the editor stays usable, and
  sums up the game's identity.

### Worlds

- A world is a persistent scene plus a World asset (`.stworld`) of layers cut into cells, each cell a scene file in a
  folder beside the world. Streaming sources (or the primary camera) load the cells within their radius, the most
  important sources first; layers can be turned off while the game plays. The Open World sample is one.
- Edit a world in place: the edit layer decides where new content goes, search `l:Props` or `cell:3,-1` in the
  Hierarchy, see an entity's cell in the Inspector, pin cells on the cell map so they stay loaded, and save only what
  changed. Cook writes each cell's entities, bodies, bounds and loading size into the world and warns about cells beyond
  20 km or entity IDs two cells share. Its cells collide by the project's collision layers, like every scene.
- Exported games wait for the cells of layers marked Load Before Start (with their collision) while the loading scene
  shows, then stream the rest.

### Rendering

- Physically based shading, with clear coat and glass, lit by HDR environment maps, a procedural sky or a physical
  atmosphere, by reflection probes baked in the editor and screen-space reflections, by clustered point and spot lights
  (up to 16 of them casting shadows) and by the directional light's cascaded soft shadows (PCSS), blended between
  cascades. Height fog, ground-truth ambient occlusion, HDR with a pre-exposure and automatic exposure, bloom, tone
  mapping, color grading, FXAA or TAA (Sharp, Original or Custom), and signed-distance-field text in the world and on
  screen. Lights are stated in lux, candela and nits.
- Textures are block-compressed on import (BC7, BC1, BC4, BC5). Meshes and textures load in the background within
  memory budgets taken from the GPU's memory; textures arrive as their smallest mip levels and refine to the resolution
  the screen needs.
- The GPU culls (view, size, occlusion) and draws the scene for the view, the shadow cascades and the shadows of point
  and spot lights, picks levels of detail by their error on screen and cross-fades them, and renders relative to the
  camera. Where a GPU cannot draw this way, the CPU culls and batches.

### Game UI

- Screen-space and world-space canvases with a reference resolution and scale modes, rects with anchors and pivots,
  images (also sliced), text, buttons, toggles, sliders, input fields, layout groups and masks, navigated with the
  mouse, the keyboard and gamepads through the input actions of the map **UI**, which the project and its players can
  rebind.
- The editor shows the UI over the scene (the viewport's Game UI chip), selects elements with a click and lays a canvas
  out on its own in the 2D mode, with zoom, pan and snapping.

### Physics, audio and animation

- box3d rigid bodies with box, sphere, capsule and mesh colliders (convex hulls, and triangle meshes on static bodies),
  triggers, collision events, raycasts and overlap queries; character controllers that climb steps and slopes, stick to
  the ground, ride moving ground and push bodies; named collision layers, the same in the whole project. The physics
  world grows in place while the game plays when more bodies arrive than it reserved room for, and the Streaming panel
  and `physics.stats` show its capacity. When the fixed steps cannot keep up with real time, the game slows down
  instead of falling further behind.
- miniaudio playback with spatial sources, a listener that follows the camera or a listener component, and one-shot
  sounds. The Asset tab shows an audio clip's waveform and plays it.
- glTF skins and animations import as skeletons, clips and skinned meshes, skinned on the GPU. Animation controllers
  (`.stanimator`) have states, parameters, cross-fades, events, root motion and joints that other entities attach to;
  the Asset tab edits them as tables, and the viewport previews poses in edit mode.

### Scripting

- Game logic is C++ classes in the project's `Scripts` folder. Scripts > New Script... starts one, Ctrl+B builds them,
  and Play builds changed scripts first. The editor opens files in your code editor (your choice in Preferences), and
  the `CMakePresets.json` it writes lets IDEs build the scripts. Rebuilding while the game plays hot-reloads them and
  keeps their fields.
- A crash in a script (an access violation, a division by zero, a stack overflow, an exception that escapes, and on
  Windows an abort) stops play mode and disables the scripts; the editor keeps running. The Problems panel lists build
  errors and the crash, on Windows with its file and line; a double click opens the line.
- The SDK covers entities, components, transforms and prefabs, input (keyboard, mouse, gamepads, cursor modes, and the
  project's input actions with rebinding, binding capture and the players' saved bindings), time (also unscaled, for
  pause menus), rigid bodies, raycasts, contacts and collision layers, character controllers, audio, animators and
  their events, scene loads and additive sub-scenes, game UI (`StrataScript/UI.h`), worlds (`StrataScript/World.h`),
  the game's window (mode, resolution, vsync), save files, random numbers and timers. Scripts reach every new rendering
  setting through components' properties. `Docs/Manual/Scripting.md` in the archive is the guide, and the samples'
  `Scripts` folders show it at work.

### Scenes and exported games

- Scenes load in the background, in the editor and in games, and their loads can be cancelled. Additive sub-scenes
  come in slices of entities per frame and unload in slices too, and prefabs spawn many instances in one call.
- An exported game is a folder with the runtime renamed after the game, a `.stgame` manifest (with the project's
  settings, which the game checks before it starts, and its identity), the game's icon, a compressed `.stpak` asset pack
  (optionally pruned to what the scenes use) that holds its scenes (world cells included) and prefabs in a binary form,
  and the script module. A loading scene can play while the start scene loads, and scripts keep save files and the
  players' input bindings in the player's data folder.
- The game's executable opens the window its manifest describes, under the game's title and icon, or runs with
  `--headless` without a window or GPU at 60 frames per second. `--frames`, `--screenshot`, `--resolution`, `--perf-log`
  (with GPU times and draw statistics) and `--asset-budget-mb` help test it.

### AI agents

- Every editor operation is a command with a JSON Schema, the same for the UI, command scripts and agents. `StrataCLI`
  calls them from the command line, and `StrataCLI mcp` offers them to MCP clients as tools, in toolsets (`settings`
  for project settings and preferences is new); the launcher shows the line that connects Claude Code. An agent can
  start an editor itself, with or without a window.
- Agents play the running game with simulated keyboard, mouse and gamepad input and input actions counted in game
  updates, whole input timelines and clicks on UI elements (`input.click`), start it paused and step it exactly, wait in
  command scripts until a query's result meets an expectation (`until`), and see the game through viewport captures
  from a camera of their own or the Game view's, which wait until what they show has loaded and settled, or show what a
  view presented as the game played. They build and check game UI (`ui.create`, `ui.layout`, `ui.hitTest`), worlds
  (`world.*`), lighting (`lighting.bakeProbes`, `lighting.extractSun`) and project settings (`project.setSettings`,
  `project.setCollisionLayers`, `project.setGameInfo`, `inputActions.*`) like any scene.
- The Activity panel lists every command and who ran it (the person, an agent by its name, a command script), and
  exports a session as a command script that repeats it. The status bar and the viewport show when an agent is connected
  and what it changed. `Skills/` in the archive holds three guides that teach an agent to make a game with the editor;
  the make-a-game guide walks through how each sample was made.

### Instruments and scale

- The Streaming and Performance panels, the status pills and the `asset.stats`, `physics.stats` and `editor.perf`
  commands show memory budgets, loads, uploads, physics capacity, world streaming and where each frame's CPU and GPU
  time goes; the new passes have GPU stages of their own (`GPU.TAA`, `GPU.Reflections`).
- A scene holds up to 1,048,575 entities; a world can hold more, as long as the cells loaded at once fit. Wave 6's
  rendering was measured on an Apple M2 Max with MoltenVK (Release builds, 1920x1080): TAA Sharp's pass takes about 0.48
  ms of GPU time (Original's 0.31 ms), screen-space reflections about 0.5 ms with every surface reflecting, and a pane
  of glass over the whole view about 1.7 ms more; the Open World's tour takes 5.65 ms of GPU time a frame instead of
  4.67 ms with the TAA, fog, atmosphere and shadowed lights it now uses. Evaluating 256 input actions of 4 bindings each
  takes about 7 microseconds a frame. [0.5.0's notes][scale-0.5.0] give the same Mac's physics numbers,
  [0.4.0's][scale-0.4.0] those for rendering, worlds and saves, and [0.3.0's][scale-0.3.0] those of the Windows
  development machine for what later waves did not change.

## Requirements

Every platform needs a GPU with a Vulkan 1.2 driver to show the editor and play games. Started without one, the editor
(and a game) shows a message that says so, what to install and where its log is. Without a GPU the editor still runs
headless (`StrataEditor --no-gpu`, for automation and exports) and games run with `--headless`.

New in 0.6.0, the GPU must support cube map arrays (Vulkan's `imageCubeArray`), which the reflection probes need. Every
desktop GPU with a Vulkan 1.2 driver has them, MoltenVK on the Mac included; a GPU without them is refused at start-up:
the editor (or the game) says that no usable graphics device was found, and the log names what the GPU lacks.

- **Windows 10 or 11, x64.** The Vulkan runtime comes with the graphics drivers of NVIDIA, AMD and Intel GPUs; update
  the driver if the editor reports that Vulkan is not available. Nothing else is needed to run Strata.
- **Linux x64: Ubuntu 24.04 or newer**, or another distribution with glibc 2.39 and the C++ library of GCC 14. The
  editor needs the Vulkan loader, a Vulkan driver, GTK 3 and the X11 or Wayland libraries (NVIDIA GPUs get Vulkan from
  NVIDIA's driver); exported games need the same without GTK. On Ubuntu:

  ```sh
  sudo apt install libvulkan1 mesa-vulkan-drivers libgtk-3-0t64 libx11-6 libxrandr2 libxinerama1 \
    libxcursor1 libxi6 libxkbcommon0 libwayland-client0
  ```

- **macOS 14 or newer, Apple silicon.** Vulkan runs on Metal through MoltenVK, which the archive does not include:
  install the Vulkan SDK from https://vulkan.lunarg.com/sdk/home#mac (choose "System Global Installation"), or run
  `brew install molten-vk vulkan-loader`.

**Building scripts** needs a C++ compiler and CMake 3.25 or later. The editor looks for them at the first script build
and says exactly what is missing:

- Windows: Visual Studio 2022 (17.10 or later) or 2026 with the "Desktop development with C++" workload, or the Build
  Tools for Visual Studio with the C++ build tools. Its C++ CMake tools (installed with the workload by default) provide
  CMake; a CMake from https://cmake.org/download/ works too.
- Linux: `sudo apt install g++ cmake ninja-build`.
- macOS: `xcode-select --install` (the Command Line Tools), and `brew install cmake ninja` or CMake.app from
  https://cmake.org/download/.

The samples' scripts are built the same way, so opening a sample and pressing Play needs them too.

## First start

The executables are not signed, so the system asks before it runs them the first time.

- **Windows:** before unpacking, right-click the downloaded `.zip`, choose Properties and tick **Unblock**, so the
  unpacked files (and the games you export, which copy the runtime) are not marked as downloaded. Otherwise SmartScreen
  shows "Windows protected your PC" when you start the editor: choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
- **macOS:** remove the quarantine mark from the unpacked folder once, in Terminal, then start `./StrataEditor` in it:
  `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine ~/Downloads/Strata-0.6.0-macos-arm64` (use the folder's real path).
- **Linux:** nothing to do; start `./StrataEditor` in the folder.

## Known issues

### Platforms

- **Waves 4 to 6 were developed and tested on a Mac**, while the Windows machine Strata had been developed on was away.
  CI builds every platform and runs the tests that need no GPU (physics, the samples' play-throughs, project settings
  and input actions included), but its machines have no GPU: the renderer of 0.4.0 (GPU culling and indirect drawing,
  the shadow, light and level-of-detail changes, the game UI pass), everything 0.6.0 added to it (TAA, the shadows of
  point and spot lights, reflection probes, screen-space reflections, fog, the atmosphere, clear coat and glass) and the
  samples' windowed runs have run only on Apple's MoltenVK, not yet on a Windows or Linux GPU. NVIDIA, AMD and Intel
  drivers also take paths that MoltenVK cannot (draw counts the GPU writes, min/max samplers for the occlusion
  pyramids), which have not run anywhere yet. Rendering problems on Windows and Linux are especially worth reporting
  (see [Reporting problems](#reporting-problems)). The editor has not been looked at on a Windows display at 150% since
  0.3.0.
- **macOS:** the editor, its rendering, the samples and exported games have been run on one Mac (Apple M2 Max, macOS 26,
  MoltenVK 1.4), where every GPU test passes; other Macs and macOS 14 and 15 have not been tried. MoltenVK and the
  Vulkan loader must be installed separately (see [Requirements](#requirements)). Metal has no multi-draw, so MoltenVK
  turns each indirect draw into a draw of its own: scenes of thousands of different meshes cost more there (10,000
  distinct meshes take about 4.8 ms a frame on the M2 Max). The Performance panel's GPU time of the UI pass may be
  imprecise on Apple GPUs. An exported game is not an app bundle: the Finder shows no icon or version for it, only the
  Dock while it runs.
- **Linux:** the build passes the headless tests in CI; the windowed editor has had less use on Linux than on the other
  platforms. Some platform code has only been compiled and tested by CI there: the editor window's handling of keyboard
  layouts, cursor shapes and the clipboard, the system fonts for Chinese, Japanese and Korean text, the default project
  folder in the home directory and finding an external code editor by name. The export writes the game's desktop entry
  but does not install it, and no Linux desktop has shown one yet (the tests check the file); it names the folder's
  absolute paths, so export again after moving the folder.
- **Windows:** script builds in the system's temporary folder or under a short (8.3) path list MSBuild's warnings
  MSB8029 and MSB8012 in the Problems panel. The editor and exported games are windowed programs, which PowerShell does
  not wait for: pipe their output to wait for one and get its exit code, e.g. `.\StrataEditor --version | Write-Output`.
  Started from a terminal, a window leaves the terminal alone (its log is in the user data folder, see the package's
  README); `--version`, `--help` and headless or scripted runs print into it, and Ctrl+C ends those in order. The
  tests read an exported game's icon and version information back through Windows, but nobody has looked at them in
  Explorer yet.
- The executables are not signed (see [First start](#first-start)).

### Rendering

- **Screen-space reflections show only on smooth surfaces** (roughness below **Screen Space Reflections Max Roughness**,
  0.5 by default), and none of the samples has one: the Third-Person Adventure turns them on, but its kit's materials
  are rough (0.9 to 1), so they change nothing there. They reflect only what is on screen, transparent surfaces get
  none, and on clear-coated materials they replace the base's reflection, not the coat's.
- **Glass** shows only what was drawn before it: glass behind glass, and blended transparent surfaces behind glass, do
  not show through. Glass gets no ambient occlusion and casts no shadow, and its thickness is one value per material
  (there are no thickness maps).
- **TAA** keeps a few softer frames where a moving object uncovers what it hid (about 17 frames until the band is as
  smooth as the rest), cannot smooth surfaces that recede fast from the camera while they do, and Original stays soft
  in slow pans (Sharp, the default, keeps more detail). It would soften 2D games and pixel art: keep FXAA there.
- **Reflection probes are baked in the editor**, never while the game runs: they show the scene as it was baked, so a
  moving sun, a day-night cycle or an opening door does not change them. At most 16 probes in view light a scene.
- At most 16 point and spot lights in view cast shadows at a time; their shadows' softness is a filter radius, not the
  size of the lamp. A lamp's own model inside its light darkens everything around unless its **Cast Shadows** is off.
- Height fog is not volumetric (no light shafts), and the atmosphere is computed for a viewer near the ground: it does
  not haze distant terrain by itself (use height fog), and it is not meant to be seen from high altitude or space.
- Closing the editor while a probe bake imports its pictures can leave them in `Assets/Lighting/<scene>/`, unused,
  when their files were already copied: delete them in the Content Browser.
- The forward pass's new paths (cascade blending, probes, local shadows, screen-space reflections, clear coat and
  glass) cost GPU time whether a material uses them or not: the opaque pass of a scene of 100,000 cubes takes about 5%
  longer on the M2 Max than before wave 6 (1.23 ms instead of 1.15 ms).
- The costs of the new features were measured on the Mac only: the limits the performance tests hold them to are
  provisional until the Windows machine measures them.

### Project settings and input

- Opening a scene clears the undo history, the project settings' steps with it; each change was saved in the project
  file at once, so nothing is lost.
- Only the Third-Person Adventure reads its controls as input actions: the Physics Playground, the Open World and
  Tetris read keys, and no sample has a menu for rebinding yet (scripts can make one).
- Input actions have no processors (inverting, sensitivity, curves), modifiers or chords; mouse motion and the scroll
  wheel are not action sources (scripts read them directly); all players share one set of actions (no devices per
  player for local multiplayer); and the names of keys a game shows do not follow the player's keyboard layout.
- Gamepads are still read while an exported game is in the background, as in 0.5.0.
- Scripts cannot read the game's title or version.
- The fixed timestep and gravity remain settings of each scene, not of the project.

### Samples

- The samples take about 32 MB of the unpacked archive (17 MB of it the Open World's). To keep them that small their
  textures are 1K, and the archives hold the assets' sources but not the editor's import cache: a copy imports its
  models and textures the first time it opens.
- The Third-Person robot's facial expressions are morph targets, which the importer skips (it says so once): its face
  stays neutral. The Third-Person Adventure has no music and the Open World no sound: no CC0 loop that fit was found.
- The Open World loads no distant vegetation (there is no far version of cells yet): a light height fog hazes the
  distance, and its streaming distances and the camera's far plane keep the edge far away.

### Editor

- An idle editor runs at 30 frames per second (10 without focus), so it answers the first input or automation request
  after a pause on its next frame, up to 33 ms later (100 ms unfocused).
- The Inspector reads every selected entity's values each frame: selecting 100,000 entities adds about 8 ms to a frame.
- The color picker clamps HDR colors (channels above 1) while you drag in it; the channel fields keep such values.
- In a 1600x900 window the default layout's bottom area shows one row of Content Browser tiles.
- The Asset tab's audio preview has only been checked on the null audio device, not by ear.
- A scene's loading progress can jump when a large texture's smallest mip levels arrive.
- There is no prefab edit mode: change a prefab through an instance and apply it. Prefabs and the Asset tab's editors
  (materials, import settings, controllers) still save in the frame, not in the background as scenes do.
- At a fixed resolution the Game view gives the game the editor's UI scale as its display's content scale, so canvases
  set to Constant Physical Size can show at another size than in the exported game.
- An `input.sequence` step that fails a check only its command can make (an unknown key name, a click that hits
  nothing) stops the sequence and resumes the game, but the step's earlier events have already reached the game.

### Worlds and game UI

- Keep worlds within 20 km of the origin: positions are 32-bit floats, precise to about 2 mm there. Distant cells are
  not drawn at all (there are no simplified far versions yet): match the height fog or the camera's far plane to the
  layers' streaming distances.
- Moving bodies belong to the cell they loaded with and unload with it, so objects that roam the whole world belong in
  the persistent scene; references between entities of different cells resolve only while both cells are loaded; prefab
  changes reach a cell's instances when the cell loads in the editor; a layer's cell size cannot change once it has
  cells.
- If the editor stops in the middle of saving a world, new cells it wrote can be missing from the world's list; the
  recovery journal keeps their edits, and the next save lists them.
- World-space canvases are drawn unlit, without shadows, after post-processing, and the game's clicks on them do not
  check for scene geometry in front (the editor's picks do). The 2D mode edits screen-space canvases only.

### Scripts and engine

- Creating or removing many bodies at once takes longer than it did on Jolt (0.4.0), because box3d adds every shape to
  its trees one at a time on the main thread: on the M2 Max 100,000 static bodies take about 98 ms instead of 29 ms,
  65,536 bodies created in one frame hold it for 73-80 ms, and the longest frame of unloading a 25,000-entity world cell
  is about 4 ms instead of 2.6 ms. Every body still gets its collision at once (none is left without it for frames), so
  a large spawn or a dense cell costs one longer frame. Spawn in smaller groups over several frames where that matters.
- Triggers do not see triangle-mesh colliders (box3d's sensors see convex shapes only), and a triangle-mesh collider
  placed away from its trigger body's origin cannot be used (a warning says so).
- box3d is young: its first release, 0.1.0, came out in June 2026, and Strata uses a commit of 2026-10-15 from after
  it. Its mover, on which the character controllers are built, is marked experimental there. Physics may behave a
  little differently when later versions of box3d come.
- box3d holds at most 128 physics worlds at once (one per scene that simulates): a scene beyond them plays without
  physics and logs an error.
- On Linux and macOS a script crash names the function and its offset in the module, not the file and line.
- Scripts are native code, so not every failure can be contained: an endless loop in a script blocks the editor or the
  game, and on Linux and macOS `abort()` (also from a failed `assert`) ends the process.
- Hot reload keeps only a script's fields: a `SubScene` kept in another member is reset, so keep its ID in a field.
- A mesh reimported while not loaded leaves its colliders' old shape until it loads.
- Root motion that drives a character controller is applied at the frame's first fixed step. On a tilted character its
  vertical part becomes the controller's velocity, and gravity and the ground then decide the height.
- HDR textures are stored uncompressed (RGBA16F) until a BC6H encoder exists.
- Packs store small assets one by one: a pack of 30,000 materials is about 10 times the size of their content.
- Builds from source can leave out physics, audio and animation, but not yet scripting.

### Not in this release

Planned for later waves: render scaling, dynamic resolution and TAA as an upscaler, with quality presets and renderer
defaults for the whole project; volumetric fog and light shafts; more than 16 shadowed lights, soft shadows sized by
the lamp and contact shadows; reflection probes captured while the game runs; crash reports in shipped games, exports
per platform with a macOS app bundle, and signed exports; input processors, chords and local multiplayer; a manual in
the Help menu, playable templates, particles, trails and decals, simplified far versions of world cells (HLOD), terrain
with sculpting and painting and height-field colliders, previews of animators and clips, command scripts and simulated
input for exported games, worlds beyond 20 km (origin rebasing), compact packs of small assets, OBJ and FBX import,
morph targets, physics joints, ragdolls and vehicles (on box3d's joints), triggers that see triangle meshes, box3d's
recordings and replays as a debugging tool, an audio mixer with buses and effects, audio streamed from packs,
localization, panels outside the main window, orthographic views and surface snapping, and a prefab edit mode.

## Reporting problems

Report problems at https://github.com/2042Third/GameEngine/issues with:

- the version: Help > About Strata, or `StrataEditor --version`;
- your platform and GPU (Help > About Strata shows the GPU and the driver);
- what you did, step by step, and what happened;
- the log: `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Strata\Logs\StrataEditor.log` on Windows, `~/.local/share/Strata/Logs` on Linux and
  `~/Library/Application Support/Strata/Logs` on macOS. Exported games log to `Logs/Game.log` in a folder named after
  the game, beside the `Strata` folder.
