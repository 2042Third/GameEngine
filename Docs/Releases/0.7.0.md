# Strata 0.7.0

Strata is a 3D game engine written in C++20, with an editor that people and AI agents drive through the same commands,
game logic written as C++ scripts that the editor builds, and exported games that run in StrataRuntime. 0.7.0 holds
what development wave 7 delivered, integrated on 2026-10-28, on top of everything in [0.6.0][notes-0.6.0] (wave 6),
[0.5.0][notes-0.5.0] (wave 5), [0.4.0][notes-0.4.0] (wave 4) and [0.3.0][notes-0.3.0] (waves 1 to 3). Versions follow
the waves (0.8.0 will be wave 8), and patch versions carry fixes.

the exposure and the materials' light changed for every scene, and projects draw at the new High quality unless they
choose another (see [Upgrading from 0.6.0](#upgrading-from-060)). Their glTF models import once more in the background.
Scripts build against the same SDK, which only gained functions; export games again to get 0.7.0's runtime and the
graphics options.

## What's new in 0.7.0

Wave 7 took on the image. Seen at 3840x2160 on the Windows machine Strata is developed on, 0.6.0's shadows were
pixelated (one shadow texel spanned 2.2 to 8.7 screen pixels) and grainy without TAA, every scene needed +1 EV because
the tone curve showed middle gray too dark, the template's sky lit it like an overcast day, rough metals lost up to 69%
of their light, and nobody could choose how much the renderer spends. The wave's direction: realistic, filmic images at
native resolution (no upscaling, nothing that needs temporal accumulation to look right), restrained by default.

- **Graphics quality you can choose.** **Project Settings > Graphics Quality** shows the presets Low, Medium (0.6.0's
  costs), High (the new default) and Ultra side by side, a row per setting with what it costs in memory and GPU time,
  and a Custom column you edit. Games, the editor's views and captures follow it and change it while they run; the
  Scene view's quality chip can use a preset of its own on your computer, and `<Game> --quality low` plays one run at a
  preset. No setting renders below the screen's resolution. `Docs/Manual/Graphics.md` explains each row.
- **Graphics options in your games.** Scripts offer the players a Graphics menu through `StrataScript/Graphics.h`
  (`Graphics::SetPreset`, `Graphics::Set` by a row's name, `Graphics::Reset`, `Graphics::Save`, and
  `Graphics::Options()` with each row's title, description and choices); a player's choice is kept beside the game's
  saves and loaded at the next start. Players can also choose the anti-aliasing method and turn off motion blur, depth
  of field, film grain and chromatic aberration whatever the scene uses. The Third-Person Adventure's settings menu has
  a Graphics row.
- **Shadows of a real sun.** The sun's shadows are as fine as the screen where they fall (at 3840x2160 on High the
  samples have about one shadow texel per screen pixel or finer): the cascades follow what the camera sees and shrink to
  the surfaces each one shades. Their soft edges are as wide as a sun of the light's **Shadow Softness** makes them
  (0.53 degrees, the real sun's, by default): sharp where an object touches the ground, softer with distance, without
  grain and the same in every frame with or without TAA, as the shadow is filtered once per pixel in a screen-space pass
  with an edge-aware blur. **Contact shadows** ground small objects and feet the shadow maps are too coarse for (at High
  and Ultra; **Contact Shadow Length** on the Directional Light). The viewport's **Shadows** debug view colors every
  surface by how many screen pixels a shadow texel spans there.
- **The image of a camera.** **Filmic**, a tone curve of Strata's own, is the default of new Post Process components, of
  scenes without one and of the Basic 3D template: a metered middle gray shows as middle gray, highlights roll off to
  white without skewing their hue, and shadows keep the scene's colors. Automatic exposure adapts like an eye (to
  brighter light twice as fast as to darker), meters the image's center more than its edges, and lets interiors read
  darker than sunlight. The output is dithered, so skies and dark falloffs no longer band; bloom keeps its size at every
  resolution; the vignette is a lens's fall-off. Post Process gains white balance and per-channel slope, offset and
  power, color lookups from `.cube` files, motion blur (off by default), film grain, chromatic aberration and lens
  flares (each off by default), and a morphological anti-aliasing that keeps texture detail without TAA. A Camera gains
  a physical lens for depth of field (focus distance or a focus entity, an aperture as an f-number, a sensor height).
  `Docs/Manual/Camera.md` explains them with recipes for a sunny day, an interior and a night.
- **Believable light.** New projects open under the physical atmosphere with a 120,000 lux sun 40 degrees up and a sun
  to sky ratio of about 8, as on a clear day. Rough metals keep their light (energy compensation: a rough metal reflects
  up to three times what it did), highlights hold at 4K without sparkle, and ambient occlusion is denoised within the
  frame: the same image with TAA, FXAA or no anti-aliasing, with a 1 m radius and multi-bounce for bright surfaces.
  Under the atmosphere, distance fades into the air's haze (aerial perspective). **Sun and sky presets** (Clear, Hazy,
  Overcast, Golden Hour) set a sky and a sun of real values in one step, and five **lighting debug views** (lighting,
  ambient occlusion, indirect, luminance in EV and albedo) show and measure a scene's light. At High and Ultra
  screen-space reflections trace every pixel at full resolution and need no TAA.
- **Physics that runs fast and settles.** Strata's own work around each physics step fell by a quarter to two fifths in
  busy scenes (70,000 falling boxes: from 0.78 to 0.47 of box3d's own step), contacts nobody listens to are no longer
  looked up, and a streamed cell that unloads no longer removes its static bodies in that frame. **Project Settings >
  Physics > Simulation** tunes how bodies settle (contact recycling, the sleep threshold, the contacts' stiffness,
  damping and push speed), changeable while the game plays; piles of balls fall asleep a quarter to a third sooner, and
  character controllers and moving platforms no longer reuse stale contacts. `physics.stats`, `editor.status`, the
  runtime's perf log and the Streaming panel show what every step does.
- **A faster editor.** The Performance panel costs a frame less than half what it did, the Content Browser's first
  thumbnails no longer hitch it, editing and playing scenes without mesh renderers costs about a sixth less per frame,
  and opening a large scene rarely freezes frames any more.
- **The samples show it.** The Third-Person Adventure, the Open World and the Physics Playground have the sun's real
  softness, Filmic at exposures measured for their scenes, the lens vignette and ambient occlusion of 1 m; the
  Playground stands under a clear sky, the Open World's sun is white, its leaves are green again and its distance hazes.
  Their pictures and screenshots were taken again.
- **Smaller changes and fixes.** Agents get `graphics.resetPlayerOptions`, `lighting.applySkyPreset`,
  `physics.setDetailedStats`, `viewport.capture {quality}` and the debug views' statistics in `viewport.capture`, and
  `Tools/LookDev.py` captures fixed pictures of the samples for comparison. Exported games run for a span of game time
  with `--seconds`. Fixed: the atmosphere could leave mirrors black after the sky switched to another source and back
  until the sun moved; a validation layer the loader lists but cannot load kept a device from starting on Windows;
  captures used half the point and spot lights' shadow atlas.

## Upgrading from 0.6.0

- **Projects draw at High.** A project without a Graphics Quality setting uses High: at 1440 output rows and more, the
  sun's shadows go into 4096-texel cascades (256 MiB per view with a shadowed sun instead of 64), and at every size High
  adds a wider filter for soft shadows, contact shadows, finer ambient occlusion, a 512-texel sky reflection (12 MiB
  more per view, 24 under the atmosphere) and, where a Post Process turns screen-space reflections on, a full-resolution
  trace (about 232 MiB per view at 3840x2160 instead of 79). Choose **Medium** to keep 0.6.0's costs.
- **How 0.6.0's scenes look.** Stored values are kept, but the shading changes:
  - The sun's penumbrae are half as wide for the same Shadow Softness (set it to twice its old value to keep a scene's
    width), narrow edges are filtered by a fixed tent instead of rotated noise, the cascades fit the view, and contact
    shadows appear at High and Ultra, also under meshes whose Cast Shadows is off (set **Contact Shadow Length** to 0 on
    a light to keep its old look).
  - The output is dithered, FXAA blends less (0.25 instead of 0.75 of its subpixel smoothing), bloom keeps its share of
    the image at 4K, and the vignette follows a lens: a stored 0.2 now darkens a 60 degree view's corners by 16.5%
    instead of 20%. Scenes that stored ACES keep it; a Post Process moved to Filmic needs about 0.8 EV less exposure
    compensation.
  - Automatic exposure meters center-weighted, adapts to brighter scenes twice as fast and darkens scenes that meter
    below the directional light's gray card. For 0.6.0's exposure set **Auto Exposure Metering** to Average, **Auto
    Exposure Key** to 0 and **Auto Exposure Light Speed** to the scene's **Auto Exposure Speed**.
  - Metals keep their energy (a metal of roughness 1 reflects up to three times what it did; dielectrics' specular grows
    about 9%), ambient occlusion gains multi-bounce (turn **Ambient Occlusion Multi Bounce** off for 0.6.0's darker
    creases), and scenes under the atmosphere gain aerial perspective (turn **Aerial Perspective** off on the Sky Light
    to keep 0.6.0's look).
- **Physics.** Bodies fall asleep below 0.08 m/s instead of 0.05 (set Physics.Simulation's Sleep Threshold to 0.05 for
  the old behavior). Character controllers and kinematic bodies no longer reuse contacts, so what they carry or push
  meets them where they are.
- **One more import.** The glTF importer gained **Base Color Factors Are sRGB**, so every glTF model imports once more
  in the background the first time 0.7.0 opens a project, and asset thumbnails are made again.
- **Scripts.** The script ABI keeps version 1: 0.7.0 appends seven host functions for graphics options (162 in all) and
  removes none, so script modules built for 0.6.0 load unchanged, and modules that use the options also run on 0.6.0
  (the calls do nothing there).
- **Exported games** keep the runtime they were exported with: export again to get 0.7.0's runtime, the project's
  Graphics Quality and the players' options.
- **Sample copies.** A copy of a sample made with 0.6.0 stays that copy: open a new copy to get 0.7.0's look.

## What you can do with it

### Editor

- Start from the launcher: New Project (an empty project, or the Basic 3D template: a lit scene with a camera, a sun and
  the physical atmosphere, Filmic and TAA, and a ground with a collider), Open Project, Open Sample (the Third-Person
  Adventure, the Open World, the Physics Playground and Tetris, games an AI agent made through the editor's commands)
  and the recent projects, each with a picture of its scene; File > Preferences... opens there too.
- Build scenes in dockable panels: the Scene view (gizmos, snapping, a quality chip, the Streaming, LOD, Mip, Motion,
  Shadows and lighting debug views, cell outlines in worlds) and the Game view, Hierarchy (multi-selection, drag and
  drop, rename in place, filters, UI presets, a world's layers and cells), Inspector (edits every selected entity at
  once, groups Post Process, light and camera properties under headings, marks prefab overrides, shows the scene's
  settings with its sky, sky presets, fog, sun extraction and probe bakes), Asset tab (materials, import settings,
  previews, worlds' layers), Content Browser, Console, Problems, Activity, Streaming (with the world's cell map and
  physics), Performance and Project Settings (Graphics Quality and Physics Simulation among its pages). Every edit can
  be undone; the scene plays, pauses, steps and simulates in the editor.
- Drop files on the window to import them: glTF 2.0 models (also meshopt-compressed, with clear coat and glass), PNG,
  JPEG, TGA, BMP, PSD, GIF and Radiance HDR textures, `.cube` color lookups, WAV, MP3, FLAC and Ogg Vorbis audio, and
  TrueType fonts. Imports run in the background, within a memory budget.
- The Content Browser has a folder tree, grid and list views, filters, drag and drop, and thumbnails of textures,
  materials, meshes, models and prefabs. Deleting an asset that something uses is refused; a delete moves the files to
  the project's trash, and undo restores them.
- New content rests on the surface under the view's pivot or the cursor, and End drops the selection to the ground.
  Prefab instances stay linked and keep their overrides: apply and revert them per property or whole, and unpack them.
- Scenes, worlds and the project file save in the background. Unsaved changes are journaled a few seconds after each
  edit and offered for recovery after a crash.
- File > Export Game... writes the game into a folder of its own in the background, while the editor stays usable.

### Worlds

- A world is a persistent scene plus a World asset (`.stworld`) of layers cut into cells, each cell a scene file in a
  folder beside the world. Streaming sources (or the primary camera) load the cells within their radius, the most
  important sources first; layers can be turned off while the game plays. The Open World sample is one.
- Edit a world in place: the edit layer decides where new content goes, search `l:Props` or `cell:3,-1` in the
  Hierarchy, pin cells on the cell map so they stay loaded, and save only what changed. Cook writes each cell's
  entities, bodies, bounds and loading size into the world.
- Exported games wait for the cells of layers marked Load Before Start (with their collision) while the loading scene
  shows, then stream the rest; an unloading cell's static bodies leave the physics world over the next second.

### Rendering

- Physically based shading with energy compensation and specular anti-aliasing, clear coat and glass, lit by HDR
  environment maps, a procedural sky or a physical atmosphere with aerial perspective, by reflection probes baked in the
  editor and screen-space reflections, by clustered point and spot lights (up to 16 of them casting shadows) and by the
  directional light's cascaded shadows, fitted to the view, with a physical penumbra filtered once per pixel and contact
  shadows. Height fog, ground-truth ambient occlusion denoised within the frame, HDR with a pre-exposure and automatic
  exposure, bloom, the Filmic and other tone curves, white balance, color grading and lookups, a lens vignette, motion
  blur, depth of field, film grain, chromatic aberration, lens flares, a dithered output, FXAA, TAA (Sharp, Original or
  Custom) or morphological anti-aliasing, and signed-distance-field text in the world and on screen. Lights are stated
  in lux, candela and nits.
- Graphics Quality presets (Low, Medium, High, Ultra, Custom) scale shadows, ambient occlusion, reflections, the sky's
  reflection map, levels of detail and motion blur; nothing renders below native resolution.
- Textures are block-compressed on import (BC7, BC1, BC4, BC5). Meshes and textures load in the background within
  memory budgets taken from the GPU's memory; textures arrive as their smallest mip levels and refine to the resolution
  the screen needs.
- The GPU culls (view, size, occlusion) and draws the scene for the view, the shadow cascades and the shadows of point
  and spot lights, picks levels of detail by their error on screen and cross-fades them, and renders relative to the
  camera. Where a GPU cannot draw this way, the CPU culls and batches.

### Game UI

- Screen-space and world-space canvases with a reference resolution and scale modes, rects with anchors and pivots,
  images (also sliced), text, buttons, toggles, sliders, input fields, layout groups and masks, navigated with the
  mouse, the keyboard and gamepads through the input actions of the map **UI**, which the project and its players can
  rebind.
- The editor shows the UI over the scene (the viewport's Game UI chip), selects elements with a click and lays a canvas
  out on its own in the 2D mode, with zoom, pan and snapping.

### Physics, audio and animation

- box3d rigid bodies with box, sphere, capsule and mesh colliders (convex hulls, and triangle meshes on static bodies),
  triggers, collision events, raycasts and overlap queries; character controllers that climb steps and slopes, stick to
  the ground, ride moving ground and push bodies; named collision layers, the same in the whole project; the project's
  Physics Simulation settings for contact recycling, sleeping and the contacts. The physics world grows in place while
  the game plays when more bodies arrive than it reserved room for. When the fixed steps cannot keep up with real time,
  the game slows down instead of falling further behind.
- miniaudio playback with spatial sources, a listener that follows the camera or a listener component, and one-shot
  sounds. The Asset tab shows an audio clip's waveform and plays it.
- glTF skins and animations import as skeletons, clips and skinned meshes, skinned on the GPU. Animation controllers
  (`.stanimator`) have states, parameters, cross-fades, events, root motion and joints that other entities attach to.

### Scripting

- Game logic is C++ classes in the project's `Scripts` folder. Scripts > New Script... starts one, Ctrl+B builds them,
  and Play builds changed scripts first. Rebuilding while the game plays hot-reloads them and keeps their fields.
- A crash in a script stops play mode and disables the scripts; the editor keeps running, and the Problems panel lists
  build errors and the crash.
- The SDK covers entities, components, transforms and prefabs, input (keyboard, mouse, gamepads, cursor modes, and the
  project's input actions with rebinding), time, rigid bodies, raycasts, contacts and collision layers, character
  controllers, audio, animators and their events, scene loads and additive sub-scenes, game UI, worlds, the game's
  window, the players' graphics options (`StrataScript/Graphics.h`), save files, random numbers and timers.
  `Docs/Manual/Scripting.md` in the archive is the guide, and the samples' `Scripts` folders show it at work.

### Scenes and exported games

- Scenes load in the background, in the editor and in games, and their loads can be cancelled. Additive sub-scenes
  come in slices of entities per frame and unload in slices too, and prefabs spawn many instances in one call.
- An exported game is a folder with the runtime renamed after the game, a `.stgame` manifest (with the project's
  settings and identity), the game's icon, a compressed `.stpak` asset pack and the script module. Scripts keep save
  files, the players' input bindings and graphics options in the player's data folder.
- The game's executable opens the window its manifest describes, or runs with `--headless` without a window or GPU.
  `--quality`, `--frames`, `--seconds`, `--screenshot`, `--resolution`, `--perf-log` (with GPU times, draw statistics
  and physics) and `--asset-budget-mb` help test it.

### AI agents

- Every editor operation is a command with a JSON Schema, the same for the UI, command scripts and agents. `StrataCLI`
  calls them from the command line, and `StrataCLI mcp` offers them to MCP clients as tools, in toolsets; the launcher
  shows the line that connects Claude Code. An agent can start an editor itself, with or without a window.
- Agents play the running game with simulated input counted in game updates, start it paused and step it exactly, wait
  in command scripts until a query's result meets an expectation, and see the game through viewport captures at a
  quality of their choice, with the debug views' measurements (shadow texels per pixel, EV percentiles, sun to sky
  ratio, albedo shares) beside the picture. They set the project's Graphics Quality (`project.setSettings`), apply sky
  presets (`lighting.applySkyPreset`), reset the players' options (`graphics.resetPlayerOptions`) and read what physics
  does every step (`physics.stats`) like any other command.
- The Activity panel lists every command and who ran it, and exports a session as a command script that repeats it.
  `Skills/` in the archive holds the guides that teach an agent to make a game with the editor.

### Instruments and scale

- The Streaming and Performance panels, the status pills and the `asset.stats`, `physics.stats` and `editor.perf`
  commands show memory budgets, loads, uploads, physics, world streaming and where each frame's CPU and GPU time goes;
  the new passes have GPU stages of their own (`GPU.ScreenShadows`, `GPU.Aerial`, `GPU.MotionBlur`,
  `GPU.DepthOfField`), and the Graphics Quality page shows each row's stage time in the Scene view.
- Wave 7 was measured on the Windows reference machine (Intel Core i7-13700K, NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5090, Release builds,
  memory at 2133 MT/s): at 3840x2160 on High the 3D samples' scene cameras take 1.84-2.59 ms of GPU time a frame; the
  sun's screen-space shadow pass takes 0.54-0.83 ms there, and 0.11-0.16 ms at 1920x1080 on Medium; aerial perspective
  0.01 ms; motion blur 0.18 ms at 3840x2160 while the camera turns. Strata's work around a physics step of 70,000
  falling boxes takes 5.9 ms beside box3d's own 12.6. [0.6.0's notes][scale-0.6.0] give the Mac's numbers for what
  wave 7 did not change.

## Requirements

Every platform needs a GPU with a Vulkan 1.2 driver to show the editor and play games. Started without one, the editor
(and a game) shows a message that says so, what to install and where its log is. Without a GPU the editor still runs
headless (`StrataEditor --no-gpu`, for automation and exports) and games run with `--headless`. The GPU must support
cube map arrays (Vulkan's `imageCubeArray`), as in 0.6.0; 0.7.0 asks for nothing more.

- **Windows 10 or 11, x64.** The Vulkan runtime comes with the graphics drivers of NVIDIA, AMD and Intel GPUs; update
  the driver if the editor reports that Vulkan is not available. Nothing else is needed to run Strata.
- **Linux x64: Ubuntu 24.04 or newer**, or another distribution with glibc 2.39 and the C++ library of GCC 14. The
  editor needs the Vulkan loader, a Vulkan driver, GTK 3 and the X11 or Wayland libraries (NVIDIA GPUs get Vulkan from
  NVIDIA's driver); exported games need the same without GTK. On Ubuntu:

  ```sh
  sudo apt install libvulkan1 mesa-vulkan-drivers libgtk-3-0t64 libx11-6 libxrandr2 libxinerama1 \
    libxcursor1 libxi6 libxkbcommon0 libwayland-client0
  ```

- **macOS 14 or newer, Apple silicon.** Vulkan runs on Metal through MoltenVK, which the archive does not include:
  install the Vulkan SDK from https://vulkan.lunarg.com/sdk/home#mac (choose "System Global Installation"), or run
  `brew install molten-vk vulkan-loader`.

**Building scripts** needs a C++ compiler and CMake 3.25 or later. The editor looks for them at the first script build
and says exactly what is missing:

- Windows: Visual Studio 2022 (17.10 or later) or 2026 with the "Desktop development with C++" workload, or the Build
  Tools for Visual Studio with the C++ build tools. Its C++ CMake tools (installed with the workload by default) provide
  CMake; a CMake from https://cmake.org/download/ works too.
- Linux: `sudo apt install g++ cmake ninja-build`.
- macOS: `xcode-select --install` (the Command Line Tools), and `brew install cmake ninja` or CMake.app from
  https://cmake.org/download/.

The samples' scripts are built the same way, so opening a sample and pressing Play needs them too.

## First start

The executables are not signed, so the system asks before it runs them the first time.

- **Windows:** before unpacking, right-click the downloaded `.zip`, choose Properties and tick **Unblock**, so the
  unpacked files (and the games you export, which copy the runtime) are not marked as downloaded. Otherwise SmartScreen
  shows "Windows protected your PC" when you start the editor: choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
- **macOS:** remove the quarantine mark from the unpacked folder once, in Terminal, then start `./StrataEditor` in it:
  `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine ~/Downloads/Strata-0.7.0-macos-arm64` (use the folder's real path).
- **Linux:** nothing to do; start `./StrataEditor` in the folder.

## Known issues

### Platforms

- **Wave 7 was developed and tested on Windows with an NVIDIA GPU** (RTX 5090), where every GPU test now runs under the
  Vulkan validation layer, waves 4 to 6's rendering included, for the first time since 0.3.0. Its GPU tests have not run
  on the Mac (MoltenVK) for this release, and AMD and Intel GPUs and Linux drivers have not run them at all: rendering
  problems there are especially worth reporting (see [Reporting problems](#reporting-problems)). CI builds every
  platform and runs the tests that need no GPU. The editor has not been looked at on a Windows display at 150% since
  0.3.0.
- **macOS:** the editor, its rendering, the samples and exported games ran on one Mac (Apple M2 Max, macOS 26, MoltenVK
  1.4) up to 0.6.0; other Macs and macOS 14 and 15 have not been tried. MoltenVK and the Vulkan loader must be installed
  separately (see [Requirements](#requirements)). Metal has no multi-draw, so scenes of thousands of different meshes
  cost more there. An exported game is not an app bundle: the Finder shows no icon or version for it.
- **Linux:** the build passes the headless tests in CI; the windowed editor has had less use on Linux than on the other
  platforms. The export writes the game's desktop entry but does not install it, and it names the folder's absolute
  paths, so export again after moving the folder.
- **Windows:** script builds in the system's temporary folder or under a short (8.3) path list MSBuild's warnings
  MSB8029 and MSB8012 in the Problems panel. The editor and exported games are windowed programs, which PowerShell does
  not wait for: pipe their output to wait for one and get its exit code, e.g. `.\StrataEditor --version | Write-Output`.
- The executables are not signed (see [First start](#first-start)).

### Rendering

- **Motion blur is off in the template and every sample**, and in new Post Process components: in the measurements of
  moving edges it smeared some edges too wide and others too narrow, so it stays off until it holds. Turn it on per
  scene with **Motion Blur** on the Post Process.
- **The sun's screen-space shadow pass costs more than planned**: 0.54-0.83 ms of GPU time at 3840x2160 on High and
  0.11-0.16 ms at 1920x1080 on Medium (Medium's sample counts and resolutions are 0.6.0's, but this pass is new).
  Contact shadows can leave a 1-pixel fringe beside thin foliage.
- **FXAA** keeps 0.87 of the texture detail of an image without anti-aliasing, and morphological anti-aliasing 0.88;
  TAA Sharp keeps 0.92 and stays the 3D default. Medium's screen-space reflections still need TAA; High and Ultra's do
  not.
- **Screen-space reflections show only on smooth surfaces** (roughness below **Screen Space Reflections Max
  Roughness**), so the samples hardly show them; they reflect only what is on screen, and transparent surfaces get none.
  On High and Ultra, two pictures of the same view can differ in a few pixels of the reflections (seen on an RTX 5090:
  33 pixels of a 3840x2160 picture, by at most 7 of 255), so image comparisons of such scenes need a small tolerance.
- **Glass** shows only what was drawn before it, gets no ambient occlusion and casts no shadow, and its thickness is one
  value per material.
- **TAA** keeps a few softer frames where a moving object uncovers what it hid, and would soften 2D games and pixel art:
  keep FXAA there.
- **Reflection probes are baked in the editor**, never while the game runs: a moving sun or an opening door does not
  change them. At most 16 probes in view light a scene, and at most 16 point and spot lights in view cast shadows;
  their shadows' softness is a filter radius, not the size of the lamp.
- Height fog is not volumetric (no light shafts), and the atmosphere is computed for a viewer near the ground.
- The shading every material now gets (energy compensation, multi-bounce, specular anti-aliasing) costs the opaque pass
  up to 0.06 ms at 3840x2160.

### Project settings and input

- Opening a scene clears the undo history, the project settings' steps with it; each change was saved in the project
  file at once, so nothing is lost.
- Only the Third-Person Adventure reads its controls as input actions, and no sample has a menu for rebinding yet.
- Input actions have no processors, modifiers or chords; mouse motion and the scroll wheel are not action sources; all
  players share one set of actions; and the names of keys a game shows do not follow the player's keyboard layout.
- Gamepads are still read while an exported game is in the background. Scripts cannot read the game's title or version.
  The fixed timestep and gravity remain settings of each scene, not of the project.

### Samples

- The samples take about 32 MB of the unpacked archive (17 MB of it the Open World's). Their textures are 1K, and a
  copy imports its models and textures the first time it opens.
- The Third-Person robot's face stays neutral (the importer skips morph targets). The Third-Person Adventure has no
  music and the Open World no sound. The Open World loads no distant vegetation, and its day keeps the dim sun and moon
  it had: its exposure follows them.

### Editor

- An idle editor runs at 30 frames per second (10 without focus), so it answers the first input or automation request
  after a pause on its next frame.
- The Inspector reads every selected entity's values each frame: selecting 100,000 entities adds about 8 ms to a frame.
- Opening a scene of a million entities can still hold a frame for 1.2-6.5 ms, about every other time.
- There is no prefab edit mode: change a prefab through an instance and apply it. Prefabs and the Asset tab's editors
  still save in the frame, not in the background as scenes do.
- At a fixed resolution the Game view gives the game the editor's UI scale as its display's content scale.

### Worlds and game UI

- Keep worlds within 20 km of the origin. Distant cells are not drawn at all (there are no simplified far versions yet).
- Moving bodies belong to the cell they loaded with and unload with it; references between entities of different cells
  resolve only while both cells are loaded.
- World-space canvases are drawn unlit, without shadows, after post-processing, and the game's clicks on them do not
  check for scene geometry in front.

### Scripts and engine

- Creating or removing many bodies at once takes longer than it did on Jolt (0.4.0), because box3d adds every shape to
  its trees one at a time on the main thread (10,000 bodies take about 19 ms on the reference machine, 10 of them
  box3d's own). Spawn in smaller groups over several frames where that matters.
- A thousand walking characters take about 0.9 ms a physics step, and a few falling bodies beside tens of thousands of
  sleeping ones make box3d's own step several times slower.
- Triggers do not see triangle-mesh colliders, box3d holds at most 128 physics worlds at once, and its mover, on which
  the character controllers are built, is marked experimental there.
- On Linux and macOS a script crash names the function and its offset in the module, not the file and line. Scripts
  are native code, so an endless loop in a script blocks the editor or the game.
- HDR textures are stored uncompressed (RGBA16F) until a BC6H encoder exists, and packs store small assets one by one.
- Builds from source can leave out physics, audio and animation, but not yet scripting.

### Not in this release

Strata never renders below native resolution: there will be no render scaling below 1, dynamic resolution or temporal
upscaling. Planned for later waves: a graphical showcase sample; supersampled stills; soft shadows sized by the lamp for
point and spot lights, more sun cascades and distant shadows for open worlds; volumetric fog and light shafts, a baked
irradiance volume and reflection probes captured while the game runs; MSAA; crash reports in shipped games, exports per
platform with a macOS app bundle, and signed exports; input processors, chords and local multiplayer; a manual in the
Help menu, playable templates, particles, trails and decals, simplified far versions of world cells (HLOD), terrain with
sculpting and painting, previews of animators and clips, command scripts for exported games, worlds beyond 20 km, OBJ
and FBX import, morph targets, physics joints, ragdolls and vehicles, an audio mixer, localization, and a prefab edit
mode.

## Reporting problems

Report problems at https://github.com/2042Third/GameEngine/issues with:

- the version: Help > About Strata, or `StrataEditor --version`;
- your platform and GPU (Help > About Strata shows the GPU and the driver);
- what you did, step by step, and what happened;
- the log: `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Strata\Logs\StrataEditor.log` on Windows, `~/.local/share/Strata/Logs` on Linux and
  `~/Library/Application Support/Strata/Logs` on macOS. Exported games log to `Logs/Game.log` in a folder named after
  the game, beside the `Strata` folder.
