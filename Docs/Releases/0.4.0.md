# Strata 0.4.0

Strata is a 3D game engine written in C++20, with an editor that people and AI agents drive through the same commands,
game logic written as C++ scripts that the editor builds, and exported games that run in StrataRuntime. 0.4.0 holds
what development wave 4 delivered, completed on 2026-10-19, on top of everything in [0.3.0][notes-0.3.0] (waves 1 to
3). Versions follow the waves (0.5.0 will be wave 5), and patch versions carry fixes.

`SHA256SUMS.txt` holds the checksums of all three. To verify an archive, run `sha256sum -c <archive>.sha256` on Linux,
`shasum -a 256 -c <archive>.sha256` on macOS, or compare `(Get-FileHash <archive>).Hash` with the `.sha256` file in
PowerShell.

Each archive holds one folder, `Strata-0.4.0-<platform>`. Unpack it anywhere (on Windows, see
[First start](#first-start) first) and start `StrataEditor` in it. Its `README.md` explains the rest: making a game and
connecting an AI agent.

Projects made with 0.3.0 open in 0.4.0. The first time, the editor imports their models and textures again (their
cooked forms changed) and makes new thumbnails; export games again to get 0.4.0's runtime.

## What's new in 0.4.0

- **Worlds streamed in cells.** A world is a level too large to keep loaded whole: an island, a city, a long road. It
  is a persistent scene (the player, cameras, lights, game managers) and a World asset whose layers (Terrain,
  Buildings, Props, Gameplay, each with its own cell size and streaming distance) are cut into square cells, each a
  scene file of its own. Games load and unload the cells around their streaming sources (usually the player) within
  per-frame budgets, so no frame creates or frees a whole cell, and physics grows as cells arrive, so no streamed body
  is left without collision. In the editor, **New > World** in the Content Browser makes one; cells stream around the
  editor's camera, new content goes into the cell of the chosen edit layer, an entity moved across a cell border moves
  into the neighboring cell in the same undo step, changed cells stay loaded until saved, and Save writes only what
  changed. The Streaming panel's cell map shows every cell's state and the streaming sources, Play starts from what the
  editor has loaded, and Cook (also part of every export) measures the cells and sizes the game's physics for the
  busiest place. Scripts prepare streaming before a teleport and turn layers on and off (`StrataScript/World.h`).
  `Docs/Manual/Worlds.md` in the archive is the guide.
- **GPU-driven rendering.** The scene is kept on the GPU, which culls it for the view and for every shadow cascade (what
  is out of view, smaller than half a pixel or hidden behind other objects), chooses levels of detail and draws with
  indirect draws, skinned characters included. A frame's CPU cost no longer grows with the number of objects: on an
  Apple M2 Max, `SceneRenderer::Render` of 1,000,000 cubes takes about 0.6 ms of CPU time, and of 100,000 less than half
  of 0.3.0's time. All meshes share one geometry pool, so 10,000 different meshes draw in a few commands. Point and spot
  lights are culled on the GPU too: 10,000 in view are all drawn (0.3.0 kept the 4,096 most relevant). Imported models
  get levels of detail generated with their error, chosen so that the difference stays under a pixel, and cross-fade
  instead of popping. Rendering relative to the camera keeps a scene precise far from the origin (a view 20 km out looks
  as it does at the origin). Shadow cascades are fitted on the GPU and kept while nothing in them changes, textures
  stream for what is actually seen, keep their levels for 30 seconds after the view stops needing them, and fade sharper
  levels in. The Performance panel, `editor.perf` and `--perf-log` show the GPU's time per stage.
- **Game UI.** Menus, HUDs, pause screens and settings: canvases that scale with the screen, rects with anchors and
  pivots, images (rounded, bordered, sliced and filled), text, buttons, toggles, sliders, input fields, layout groups
  and masks, drawn in one batched pass. The mouse, the keyboard and gamepads navigate it; scripts get `OnUIClick`,
  `OnUIValueChanged`, `OnUIFocusChanged` and `OnUISubmit`, and change elements through `entity.GetUI()`; the UI keeps
  animating while the game is paused. Canvases can also stand in the world (a sign, a computer screen). The Hierarchy's
  **+ > UI** makes ready elements, the Inspector edits anchors from presets, and the viewport's **2D** mode shows a
  canvas on its own, as the game lays it out. A scene with a canvas and no camera (a main menu) shows its UI over
  black. The Tetris sample's HUD is a UI canvas now. `Docs/Manual/GameUI.md` is the guide.
- **Background saves and large projects.** A save writes a snapshot of the scene in the background while you go on
  working, and closing or quitting waits for it: a scene of a million entities saves in a few seconds without holding
  up the editor (in 0.3.0 the save froze it for 22 s on the Windows development machine). Scenes are read and written
  entity by entity, so opening a million entities takes less than half the memory (810 instead of 2,047 MB on the
  M2 Max), and loads can be cancelled. Exported games keep scenes and prefabs in a binary form that loads on worker
  threads: a million-entity world starts in 0.5 s instead of 2.2 s on the M2 Max. Projects of tens of thousands of
  assets open while their assets are scanned, and imports run within a memory budget (a quarter of the machine's
  memory, 1 to 4 GiB), so a folder of large textures no longer takes all of it.
- **The Game view.** While the game plays, the Game view shows what a player sees (the primary camera with the HUD,
  without editor overlays) beside or behind the Scene view, which keeps the editor's camera, picking and gizmos. Its
  resolution menu fills the view, keeps an aspect ratio (16:9, 16:10, 4:3, 21:9), renders fixed sizes (1920x1080,
  1280x720 or your own) or follows what the game asks for. `Docs/Manual/GameView.md` is the guide.
- **Prefab overrides.** An instance keeps values of its own (a light's intensity, a script's field, a component added
  or removed) while every other change of its prefab reaches it. The Inspector shows a prefab bar with the instance's
  overrides and marks overridden properties and components; Apply to Prefab and Revert work on one property, one
  component or the whole instance. `Docs/Manual/Prefabs.md` is the guide.
- **Exact play-testing for agents.** `play.step` answers once its steps ran, input lasts a number of the game's fixed
  updates rather than rendered frames (so a test gives the same result on any machine), and `input.sequence` plays a
  whole timeline of keys, mouse and clicks. Agents read world positions and bounds (`entity.get` with `world`,
  `entity.find` with `box`, `asset.info`), see which scene is running, and make many changes as one undo step
  (`editor.batch`, `prefab.instantiate` with `instances`). The MCP server offers the commands in toolsets: 32 tools
  by default instead of all 124 (`StrataCLI mcp --toolsets` or the `strata_toolsets` tool add more), and
  `editor.status` answers in a compact form.
- **Authoring that behaves as shown.** Skinned characters stand in their rest pose while you edit (or at the first
  frame of their animation: the viewport's Camera chip), in thumbnails and previews too, instead of lying on their
  backs. A model's import scale reaches the instances already placed. Normal maps are recognized by their file names,
  and a material that uses a texture not imported as a normal map says so, with a button that fixes it. On Retina
  displays the editor's UI has its intended size. Also: File > Open Scene, properties that have no effect in the
  current settings shown disabled with the reason, a notice when a component lacks one it needs, toasts that stay clear
  of what you click, and Hierarchy presets that come with colliders.
- **Modules and optional subsystems.** The engine starts and stops its subsystems as modules, in dependency order, and
  each layer of the engine is a library of its own. Exported games contain none of the editor's tooling (its UI
  library, the asset pipeline, the network layer): their runtime is smaller and starts fewer threads. A build from
  source can leave out physics, audio or animation (`STRATA_WITH_PHYSICS`, `STRATA_WITH_AUDIO`,
  `STRATA_WITH_ANIMATION`), and scenes keep the components of a module the build lacks. Builds and test runs are
  faster (precompiled headers, sharded tests).

## What you can do with it

### Editor

- Start from the launcher: New Project (an empty project, or the Basic 3D template: a lit scene with a camera, a sun, a
  procedural sky and a ground with a collider), Open Project, Open Sample (Tetris, a complete game an AI agent made
  through the editor's commands) and the recent projects, each with a picture of its scene.
- Build scenes in dockable panels: the Scene view (gizmos, snapping, the Streaming, LOD and Mip debug views, cell
  outlines in worlds) and the Game view, Hierarchy (multi-selection, drag and drop, rename in place, filters, UI
  presets, a world's layers and cells), Inspector (edits every selected entity at once, marks prefab overrides, shows
  the scene's settings), Asset tab (materials, import settings, previews, worlds' layers), Content Browser, Console,
  Problems, Activity, Streaming (with the world's cell map and physics) and Performance. Every edit can be undone; the
  scene plays, pauses, steps and simulates in the editor.
- Drop files on the window to import them: glTF 2.0 models (also meshopt-compressed), PNG, JPEG, TGA, BMP, PSD, GIF and
  Radiance HDR textures, WAV, MP3, FLAC and Ogg Vorbis audio, and TrueType fonts. Imports run in the background, within
  a memory budget.
- The Content Browser has a folder tree, grid and list views, filters, drag and drop, and thumbnails of textures,
  materials, meshes, models and prefabs. Deleting an asset that something uses is refused; a delete moves the files to
  the project's trash, and undo restores them. Files a save is still writing cannot be moved or deleted until it ends.
- New content rests on the surface under the view's pivot or the cursor, and End drops the selection to the ground.
  Prefab instances stay linked and keep their overrides: apply and revert them per property or whole, and unpack them;
  a prefab's changes reach every instance except where it overrides them.
- Scenes and worlds save in the background. Unsaved changes are journaled a few seconds after each edit and offered for
  recovery after a crash. The UI asks before it replaces a scene with unsaved changes, commands refuse unless forced,
  and what is discarded stays recoverable.
- File > Export Game... writes the game into a folder of its own in the background, while the editor stays usable.

### Worlds

- A world is a persistent scene plus a World asset (`.stworld`) of layers cut into cells, each cell a scene file in a
  folder beside the world. Streaming sources (or the primary camera) load the cells within their radius, the most
  important sources first; layers can be turned off while the game plays.
- Edit a world in place: the edit layer decides where new content goes, search `l:Props` or `cell:3,-1` in the
  Hierarchy, see an entity's cell in the Inspector, pin cells on the cell map so they stay loaded, and save only what
  changed. Cook writes each cell's entities, bodies, bounds and loading size into the world and warns about cells beyond
  20 km or entity IDs two cells share.
- Exported games wait for the cells of layers marked Load Before Start (with their collision) while the loading scene
  shows, then stream the rest.

### Rendering

- Physically based shading lit by HDR environment maps or a procedural sky, clustered point and spot lights, cascaded
  soft shadows (PCSS) from the directional light, ground-truth ambient occlusion, HDR with automatic exposure, bloom,
  tone mapping, color grading and FXAA, and signed-distance-field text in the world and on screen.
- Textures are block-compressed on import (BC7, BC1, BC4, BC5). Meshes and textures load in the background within
  memory budgets taken from the GPU's memory; textures arrive as their smallest mip levels and refine to the resolution
  the screen needs.
- The GPU culls (view, size, occlusion) and draws the scene for the view and the shadow cascades, picks levels of detail
  by their error on screen and cross-fades them, and renders relative to the camera. Where a GPU cannot draw this way,
  the CPU culls and batches as in 0.3.0.

### Game UI

- Screen-space and world-space canvases with a reference resolution and scale modes, rects with anchors and pivots,
  images, text, buttons, toggles, sliders, input fields, layout groups and masks, navigated with the mouse, the keyboard
  and gamepads.
- The editor shows the UI over the scene (the viewport's Game UI chip), selects elements with a click and lays a canvas
  out on its own in the 2D mode, with zoom, pan and snapping.

### Physics, audio and animation

- Jolt rigid bodies with box, sphere, capsule and mesh colliders, triggers, collision events, raycasts and overlap
  queries; character controllers that climb steps and slopes, ride moving ground and push bodies; named collision
  layers. The physics world grows while the game plays when more bodies arrive than it has room for (bodies that wait
  for room are queued, never dropped), and the Streaming panel and `physics.stats` show its capacity. When the fixed
  steps cannot keep up with real time, the game slows down instead of falling further behind.
- miniaudio playback with spatial sources, a listener that follows the camera or a listener component, and one-shot
  sounds. The Asset tab shows an audio clip's waveform and plays it.
- glTF skins and animations import as skeletons, clips and skinned meshes, skinned on the GPU. Animation controllers
  (`.stanimator`) have states, parameters, cross-fades, events, root motion and joints that other entities attach to;
  the Asset tab edits them as tables, and the viewport previews poses in edit mode.

### Scripting

- Game logic is C++ classes in the project's `Scripts` folder. Scripts > New Script... starts one, Ctrl+B builds them,
  and Play builds changed scripts first. The editor opens files in your code editor, and the `CMakePresets.json` it
  writes lets IDEs build the scripts. Rebuilding while the game plays hot-reloads them and keeps their fields.
- A crash in a script (an access violation, a division by zero, a stack overflow, an exception that escapes, and on
  Windows an abort) stops play mode and disables the scripts; the editor keeps running. The Problems panel lists build
  errors and the crash, on Windows with its file and line; a double click opens the line.
- The SDK covers entities, components, transforms and prefabs, input (keyboard, mouse, gamepads, cursor modes), time
  (also unscaled, for pause menus), rigid bodies, raycasts, contacts and collision layers, character controllers,
  audio, animators and their events, scene loads and additive sub-scenes, game UI (`StrataScript/UI.h`), worlds
  (`StrataScript/World.h`), the game's window (mode, resolution, vsync), save files, random numbers and timers.
  `Docs/Manual/Scripting.md` in the archive is the guide.

### Scenes and exported games

- Scenes load in the background, in the editor and in games, and their loads can be cancelled. Additive sub-scenes
  come in slices of entities per frame and unload in slices too, and prefabs spawn many instances in one call.
- An exported game is a folder with the runtime renamed after the game, a `.stgame` manifest, a compressed `.stpak`
  asset pack (optionally pruned to what the scenes use) that holds its scenes (world cells included) and prefabs in a
  binary form, and the script module. A loading scene can play while the start scene loads, and scripts keep save
  files in the player's data folder.
- The game's executable opens the window its manifest describes, or runs with `--headless` without a window or GPU at
  60 frames per second. `--frames`, `--screenshot`, `--resolution`, `--perf-log` (now with GPU times and draw
  statistics) and `--asset-budget-mb` help test it.

### AI agents

- Every editor operation is a command with a JSON Schema, the same for the UI, command scripts and agents. `StrataCLI`
  calls them from the command line, and `StrataCLI mcp` offers them to MCP clients as tools, in toolsets; the launcher
  shows the line that connects Claude Code. An agent can start an editor itself, with or without a window.
- Agents play the running game with simulated keyboard, mouse and gamepad input counted in game updates, whole input
  timelines and clicks on UI elements (`input.click`), step it exactly, and see it through viewport captures from a
  camera of their own or the Game view's, which wait until what they show has loaded. They build and check game UI
  (`ui.create`, `ui.layout`, `ui.hitTest`) and worlds (`world.*`) like any scene.
- The Activity panel lists every command and who ran it (the person, an agent by its name, a command script), and
  exports a session as a command script that repeats it. The status bar and the viewport show when an agent is connected
  and what it changed. `Skills/` in the archive holds three guides that teach an agent to make a game with the editor.

### Instruments and scale

- The Streaming and Performance panels, the status pills and the `asset.stats`, `physics.stats` and `editor.perf`
  commands show memory budgets, loads, uploads, physics capacity, world streaming and where each frame's CPU and GPU
  time goes.
- A scene holds up to 1,048,575 entities; a world can hold more, as long as the cells loaded at once fit. Wave 4 was
  measured on an Apple M2 Max with MoltenVK (Release builds): `SceneRenderer::Render` of 1,000,000 cubes about 0.6 ms
  of CPU time; a 25,000-entity cell unloads with its longest frame about 2.7 ms; saving a million entities takes
  2.4-6.1 s in the background; an exported world of a million entities starts in about 0.5 s.
  [0.3.0's notes][scale-0.3.0] give the numbers of the Windows development machine for what wave 4 did not change.

## Requirements

Every platform needs a GPU with a Vulkan 1.2 driver to show the editor and play games. Started without one, the editor
(and a game) shows a message that says so, what to install and where its log is. Without a GPU the editor still runs
headless (`StrataEditor --no-gpu`, for automation and exports) and games run with `--headless`.

- **Windows 10 or 11, x64.** The Vulkan runtime comes with the graphics drivers of NVIDIA, AMD and Intel GPUs; update
  the driver if the editor reports that Vulkan is not available. Nothing else is needed to run Strata.
- **Linux x64: Ubuntu 24.04 or newer**, or another distribution with glibc 2.39 and the C++ library of GCC 14. The
  editor needs the Vulkan loader, a Vulkan driver, GTK 3 and the X11 or Wayland libraries (NVIDIA GPUs get Vulkan from
  NVIDIA's driver); exported games need the same without GTK. On Ubuntu:

  ```sh
  sudo apt install libvulkan1 mesa-vulkan-drivers libgtk-3-0t64 libx11-6 libxrandr2 libxinerama1 \
    libxcursor1 libxi6 libxkbcommon0 libwayland-client0
  ```

- **macOS 14 or newer, Apple silicon.** Vulkan runs on Metal through MoltenVK, which the archive does not include:
  install the Vulkan SDK from https://vulkan.lunarg.com/sdk/home#mac (choose "System Global Installation"), or run
  `brew install molten-vk vulkan-loader`.

**Building scripts** needs a C++ compiler and CMake 3.25 or later. The editor looks for them at the first script build
and says exactly what is missing:

- Windows: Visual Studio 2022 (17.10 or later) or 2026 with the "Desktop development with C++" workload, or the Build
  Tools for Visual Studio with the C++ build tools. Its C++ CMake tools (installed with the workload by default) provide
  CMake; a CMake from https://cmake.org/download/ works too.
- Linux: `sudo apt install g++ cmake ninja-build`.
- macOS: `xcode-select --install` (the Command Line Tools), and `brew install cmake ninja` or CMake.app from
  https://cmake.org/download/.

## First start

The executables are not signed, so the system asks before it runs them the first time.

- **Windows:** before unpacking, right-click the downloaded `.zip`, choose Properties and tick **Unblock**, so the
  unpacked files (and the games you export, which copy the runtime) are not marked as downloaded. Otherwise SmartScreen
  shows "Windows protected your PC" when you start the editor: choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
- **macOS:** remove the quarantine mark from the unpacked folder once, in Terminal, then start `./StrataEditor` in it:
  `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine ~/Downloads/Strata-0.4.0-macos-arm64` (use the folder's real path).
- **Linux:** nothing to do; start `./StrataEditor` in the folder.

## Known issues

### Platforms

- **Wave 4 was developed and tested on a Mac**, while the Windows machine Strata had been developed on was away. CI
  builds every platform and runs the tests that need no GPU, but its machines have no GPU: the new renderer (GPU
  culling and indirect drawing, the shadow, light and level-of-detail changes, the game UI pass) has not yet run on a
  Windows or Linux GPU. NVIDIA, AMD and Intel drivers also take paths that MoltenVK cannot (draw counts the GPU writes,
  min/max samplers for the occlusion pyramids), which have not run anywhere yet. Rendering problems on Windows and Linux
  are especially worth reporting (see [Reporting problems](#reporting-problems)). The editor has not been looked at on a
  Windows display at 150% since 0.3.0.
- **macOS:** the editor, its rendering, the Tetris sample and exported games have been run on one Mac (Apple M2 Max,
  macOS 26, MoltenVK 1.4), where every GPU test passes; other Macs and macOS 14 and 15 have not been tried. MoltenVK and
  the Vulkan loader must be installed separately (see [Requirements](#requirements)). Metal has no multi-draw, so
  MoltenVK turns each indirect draw into a draw of its own: scenes of thousands of different meshes cost more there
  (10,000 distinct meshes take about 4.8 ms a frame on the M2 Max). The Performance panel's GPU time of the UI pass may
  be imprecise on Apple GPUs.
- **Linux:** the build passes the headless tests in CI; the windowed editor has had less use on Linux than on the other
  platforms. Some platform code has only been compiled and tested by CI there: the editor window's handling of keyboard
  layouts, cursor shapes and the clipboard, the system fonts for Chinese, Japanese and Korean text, the default project
  folder in the home directory and finding an external code editor by name.
- **Windows:** script builds in the system's temporary folder or under a short (8.3) path list MSBuild's warnings
  MSB8029 and MSB8012 in the Problems panel. The editor and exported games are windowed programs, which PowerShell does
  not wait for: pipe their output to wait for one and get its exit code, e.g. `.\StrataEditor --version | Write-Output`.
  Started from a terminal, a window leaves the terminal alone (its log is in the user data folder, see the package's
  README); `--version`, `--help` and headless or scripted runs print into it, and Ctrl+C ends those in order.
- The executables are not signed (see [First start](#first-start)).

### Editor

- An idle editor runs at 30 frames per second (10 without focus), so it answers the first input or automation request
  after a pause on its next frame, up to 33 ms later (100 ms unfocused).
- The Inspector reads every selected entity's values each frame: selecting 100,000 entities adds about 8 ms to a frame.
- The color picker clamps HDR colors (channels above 1) while you drag in it; the channel fields keep such values.
- In a 1600x900 window the default layout's bottom area shows one row of Content Browser tiles.
- The Asset tab's audio preview has only been checked on the null audio device, not by ear.
- A scene's loading progress can jump when a large texture's smallest mip levels arrive.
- There is no prefab edit mode: change a prefab through an instance and apply it. Prefabs and the Asset tab's editors
  (materials, import settings, controllers) still save in the frame, not in the background as scenes do.
- At a fixed resolution the Game view gives the game the editor's UI scale as its display's content scale, so canvases
  set to Constant Physical Size can show at another size than in the exported game.
- An `input.sequence` step that fails a check only its command can make (an unknown key name, a click that hits
  nothing) stops the sequence and resumes the game, but the step's earlier events have already reached the game.

### Worlds and game UI

- Keep worlds within 20 km of the origin: positions are 32-bit floats, precise to about 2 mm there. Distant cells are
  not drawn at all (there are no simplified far versions yet): match fog or the camera's far plane to the layers'
  streaming distances.
- Moving bodies belong to the cell they loaded with and unload with it, so objects that roam the whole world belong in
  the persistent scene; references between entities of different cells resolve only while both cells are loaded; prefab
  changes reach a cell's instances when the cell loads in the editor; a layer's cell size cannot change once it has
  cells.
- If the editor stops in the middle of saving a world, new cells it wrote can be missing from the world's list; the
  recovery journal keeps their edits, and the next save lists them.
- World-space canvases are drawn unlit, without shadows, after post-processing, and the game's clicks on them do not
  check for scene geometry in front (the editor's picks do). The 2D mode edits screen-space canvases only.
- UI navigation uses fixed keys and buttons (arrows, Enter, Space and Escape; the d-pad, the left stick, A and B) until
  input actions with rebinding come.

### Scripts and engine

- On Linux and macOS a script crash names the function and its offset in the module, not the file and line.
- Scripts are native code, so not every failure can be contained: an endless loop in a script blocks the editor or the
  game, and on Linux and macOS `abort()` (also from a failed `assert`) ends the process.
- Hot reload keeps only a script's fields: a `SubScene` kept in another member is reset, so keep its ID in a field.
- A mesh reimported while not loaded leaves its colliders' old shape until it loads.
- Root motion that drives a character controller is applied at the frame's first fixed step. On a tilted character its
  vertical part becomes the controller's velocity, and gravity and the ground then decide the height.
- HDR textures are stored uncompressed (RGBA16F) until a BC6H encoder exists.
- Packs store small assets one by one: a pack of 30,000 materials is about 10 times the size of their content.
- Builds from source can leave out physics, audio and animation, but not yet scripting.

### Not in this release

Planned for later waves: better rendering (temporal anti-aliasing, shadows of point and spot lights, reflection probes
and screen-space reflections, height fog and a physical atmosphere), Project Settings and Preferences panels, input
actions with rebinding, crash reports and quality presets in shipped games, the game's name, icon and version in the
exported executable, a macOS app bundle, a manual in the Help menu, playable templates and a 3D sample, particles,
trails and decals, simplified far versions of world cells (HLOD), terrain with sculpting and painting, previews of
animators and clips, worlds beyond 20 km (origin rebasing), compact packs of small assets, OBJ and FBX import, morph
targets, physics joints, ragdolls and vehicles, an audio mixer with buses and effects, audio streamed from packs,
localization, panels outside the main window, orthographic views and surface snapping, and a prefab edit mode.

## Reporting problems

Report problems at https://github.com/2042Third/GameEngine/issues with:

- the version: Help > About Strata, or `StrataEditor --version`;
- your platform and GPU (Help > About Strata shows the GPU and the driver);
- what you did, step by step, and what happened;
- the log: `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Strata\Logs\StrataEditor.log` on Windows, `~/.local/share/Strata/Logs` on Linux and
  `~/Library/Application Support/Strata/Logs` on macOS. Exported games log to `Logs/Game.log` in a folder named after
  the game, beside the `Strata` folder.
